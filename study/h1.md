# H1, the pair page for people

The plan's neutral job H1 (`notes/build-plan.md`, sections 0 and 4): people read pairs of lives from packs already on file, blind to the arm, and say which they would rather read on. It checks whether people choose as the model readers do. It decides only selection's (G2's) pre-registration and lifts nothing in CLAUDE.md.

    node study/h1-build.mjs                        writes dist/h1.html and study/h1-key.json
    python3 study/h1-score.py ANSWERS.txt ...      scores pasted answer blocks against the key

## The rule that picks the pairs

Written before any pair was looked at; `study/h1-build.mjs` applies it, and nothing is picked by hand.

**Round 40's busiest pack, 12 pairs** (`reports/40-reading-busiest-pack.txt`, its key and answers). A role's choice is the version its sittings `prefer`; its margin is its score for that version minus its score for the other. A pair is **clear** when every role prefers the same version and every role scored that version strictly higher. Clear pairs are ranked by the mean margin, then by pair number. To keep the arm from being guessable from the model's side, they are taken alternately from the clear pairs whose majority was the on arm and those whose majority was the off arm, strongest first, filling from whichever list remains. If fewer than 12 pairs are clear, the rest come from pairs where every role prefers the same version (ties in score allowed), ranked the same way.

**Rounds 29–34, 6 pairs that differ in length but not in kinds.** Only pairs whose two versions round 36 rebuilt exactly (`reports/36-variety-on.json` and `-off.json`, a version kept when its lines equal the pack's). Length is lines on the page, not counting the cited events shown under a line (round 35); kinds are distinct event types on the page (`distinctTypes`, round 36). A pair is eligible when its versions differ by at most 1 kind and by at least 10 lines. Eligible pairs are ranked by the difference in lines, largest first, then by round and pair; at most 2 from one round. If fewer than 6 are eligible, the bound on kinds is relaxed to 2, then 3.

**M38's null pack, 6 pairs** (`reports/M38-reading-null-pack.txt`): both versions are the same engine, so neither is an arm. Six pairs drawn by a fixed seed (`h1-null`).

**On the page.** Each pair's versions are dealt to A and B by a fixed seed (`h1-ab`), half of each source flipped. The pairs are ordered in blocks of four (round 40, length, round 40, null), each source in a seeded order (`h1-order`), so any first 4, 8 or 12 pairs keep the 2:1:1 mix. The page shows the pairs as H01–H24 and nothing of the round, the pack or the arm.

## The key

`study/h1-key.json` maps each pair on the page to its source pack and pair, which page version is which arm, the model readers' majority (in the page's letters), the lines and kinds of each version, and the length difference. It goes to nobody who reads the page; scripts read it, people do not.

## What it reports

`study/h1-score.py`: per reader, agreement with the model readers' majority on round 40's clear pairs, with a Wilson 95% interval (and on every pair where the model had a majority, beside it); whether length predicts the choice once kinds are counted (the choice of A regressed on the differences in lines and kinds, over all pairs and readers); and the lean on the null pairs (the share choosing B, and the share choosing the longer version). There is no pass mark: at a dozen pairs, a reader at chance reaches 8 of 12 one time in five.

## Where the reader is blind

Øystein knows what round 40 added and can often tell its arm by content: the report counts round 40's pairs as not blind for him. The length and null pairs are where he is truly blind.

The pages are the packs' pages as the model readers read them, about 4,300 words a pair. A pair takes several minutes, so the page keeps progress on the device and lets a reader send what they have at any point.
