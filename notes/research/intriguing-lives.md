# What makes a life intriguing

_Research, 22 October 2026, agreed by Øystein after PR #85. What makes a simulated life one a reader wants to follow, which kinds of situation make stories, and which belong to the engine. Exit points 5, 2 and 1. Evidence, with every source, in `sources/intrigue-*.md`: our readings re-analysed (`-readings`), games and emergent narrative (`-games`), the psychology (`-science`), and situation kinds audited on 20 throwaway worlds (`-situations`, output in `intrigue-scripts/analyse-out.txt`). Reviewed by Fable 5.1 (`sources/review-intrigue-fable.md`); its two blocking findings are fixed here._

**The guard, a hard rule.** Storiness must not become scripting. No decision may read a story measure, or a count of its own kind of event for pacing; patterns are found only afterwards. Every proposal below names the state in people that produces it.

## What our readings say

The 14 readings on file give 276 pairs and 552 pages, z-scored within each reading so rounds 29–41 and M38 compare by rank.

- **Kinds of thing decide most of the score.** Lines and kinds explain R² 0.44. Eighteen story features coded from the page (a cast, a feud, a want with a setback, a secret, a reversal, an open end, callbacks, a choice against the grain) add 0.03 together.
- **One feature holds in every test: a turn in the person's own life**: a new job, a partner gained or lost, a birth, a move. Within pairs, with lines, kinds and the arm held, it goes with **+0.59 z [+0.31, +0.86]** (+0.47 before the rule was tightened once, after a first look), worth three to eight kinds. In size-matched pairs where one version read clearly better, only the winner had a turn 8 times, only the loser once.
- **Read it as an arc, not a line.** The two versions come from different worlds, so a turn marks a life that diverged. A move, the one turn that is a bare line, shows nothing (−0.08, ns); a birth, a line nobody quotes, shows most (+0.43), which can only be the family life around it. Pages with a partner or household turn carry more conflict and more people. A mechanism that adds a turn line without the life around it should expect little.
- **No sign of the persistent counterpart.** Where the readings can test it, it shows nothing or the reverse: one person in five or more years +0.05 across lives and +0.15 within pairs (ns); the same opponent in three or more years −0.06; in matched pairs the loser had that opponent more often (8 against 3). Secrets, open ends and reversals show nothing; secrets lean negative, their lines among the most formulaic on the page.
- **Readers mark errors but score sameness.** Within a reader and a page, a repeat fault goes with −0.25 z and an "out of nowhere" with −0.17 on the old brief; −0.11 and −0.05 on M38's. Unfit reasons, false lines and wording go with about nothing.
- **On the old brief, praise went to rare acts that cost something:** losing out, a job turn, being let down, a partner turn, quoted as good reasons at 1.8 to 2.4 times the average rate, while band routine and the festival were quoted almost never. On M38's brief (96 pages) the pattern fades.
- **The best lives on file** (Oliver Sayer, round 40; Arthur, round 31; Greta, round 41), chosen after the scores, share a shape: a named rival or partner across the decade, a marriage under strain, and a want that lands late as a turn and costs something at once. Greta wanted the skipper's job for 18 years, got it, froze at the first meeting, and her rival confronted her in front of everyone. Her other version, the same size, scored 1.7 points lower; Arthur's, 1.0.
- **What it cannot see.** The busiest adults' pages almost all have a cast and a conflict (98–99%), so their presence cannot be tested. All readers are one model.

## What others did, and where it went wrong

- **Talk of the Town:** beliefs that spread and mutate, about hair colour. Knowledge with nothing at stake made no intrigue. Ryan: raw simulation "will almost always lack story structure", and an act chosen by many weighted factors has a cause too diffuse to tell.
- **Crusader Kings:** rivals become nemeses; secrets and hooks are held and used later. Its events recycle within two decades.
- **Dwarf Fortress, The Sims:** the story is picked out of a noisy log by the player, or authored by them.
- **Versu** let only the player push a character against its preference, so no one else changed. **PsychSim** explains well, with goal weights fitted by hand. **Nemesis** captains are promoted _because_ they killed you: a named cause is what players retell.
- **Directors are the counter-example:** RimWorld's storytellers and Left 4 Dead's director make pacing players love, pacing that belongs to no one. Without one we accept lulls and let the page choose.
- **Coded retellings** (40, plus a Prom Week ablation with n = 2): up to three reversals read best. With random choices in place of social reasoning, reversals rose and coherence fell. More events is not more story.

## The science, used

- **Cause and consequence make an event count.** Events on a causal chain are judged more important and recalled better, rising with the number of links; dead ends drop out (replicated since 1985).
- **Interest needs the new and the understandable together** (Silvia), and is unrelated to pleasantness: a grim life can grip.
- **A breach in a small dose:** about one per folk tale is optimal. A rare event needs a chain of ordinary causes behind it, or it reads as false (Labov).
- **Surprise satisfies when its cause is already on the page.** "Recognised afterwards" means the reader can promote an earlier line to the explanation. Suspense needs a want, a person followed and long odds.
- **Weak spots.** No study measures what makes another person's ordinary life intriguing. McAdams' redemption and contamination concern the teller's well-being, and the redemptive self is an American script: a shape to allow, not a target.

## Which situations make stories

Fifteen kinds recur across Polti, Tobias, Booker, Propp, Labov, the sifting patterns and the games. **All fifteen are the engine's:** they need people, ties, wants, beliefs and time. A domain supplies the thing contested and, mostly, a public scoreboard and calendar of stakes, which a setting may declare and never paces.

What recurs across the sources are **triads**: two people and a third term (a good, a person, a secret, a debt) whose claims cannot all be met. The engine's situations are mostly pairs, settled within the month. The audit, per world-decade:

- **Linked:** succession (78–80% lead to a turn, 98% known), death (62%, reaching 4.1 people), making up (79%).
- **Upkeep:** promises, 100 a world-decade, lead somewhere 7–12% of the time, 3–6% known. Owning up happens 27 times and leads nowhere, ever, because no situation reads it.
- **Unlinked:** 56% of blows are followed by a rise within three years; 3% of those rises descend from the blow. The want records no cause, so the record may be losing the chain. Check first.
- **Thin or missing, with the state already there:**
  - **Sides.** A rivalry, split or betrayal with two or more friends of both comes 3.9 times a world-decade (22.5 counting friendships ending). No third person ever chooses between them.
  - **Secrets.** 29.5 secret acts a world-decade; 12.7% of adult-months carry one. Only a confidant can act on it (tell or confront, 3.1 a decade, 6% known).
  - **Debts from real acts.** `p.owes` is never read. Costly help (rescues 2.9, stepping up 9.7) and wrongs answered at once (retaliation 30.6) are the would-be sources.
  - **A third person.** No triangle, affair or jealousy exists in the source; 12% of married partners are thinking about leaving at any time.

## For this engine, a life is intriguing when it has

From the readings:

1. **An arc that turns:** a job, partner or family life that changes, with the life around it.
2. **Little sameness, and nothing from nowhere:** routine folds (`language.md`), and a turn has a cause the page already showed.

From the literature and games, not yet supported by the readings:

3. **Links:** a consequence after the turn. Today two thirds of knowable turns follow nothing (`reports/density-22oct.md`).
4. **A third term held across time:** a few named others recur because something stands between them. The proxies on file are null; this is a hypothesis each round tests on its own counter.
5. **Surprise from the person's own draw,** explained by an earlier line, with few reversals, never counted in order to make them.

## This fails if …

1. **Drama rises by adding events, not links.** Sizing check: the share of weighty events cited by a later one, not counting the new acts' citations of their own triggers, must rise beyond the off arm's 20-world interval. Stop if it does not.
2. **The new situations are upkeep.** Sizing check: the share of side-taking, calling-in or exposure decisions that end or start a tie, or are cited by a weighty event, within three years. Stop below 15% (the hat: 2%); on about 60 side-taking decisions in 20 worlds that is ±9 points, so size on 40 worlds. Stop too if removing the new state flips under 5% of the decisions it is weighed in.
3. **Turns arrive from nowhere.** Sizing check, with item 1's counter: at least 70% of the new turns (the engine's `TURNS`) have a cause on an earlier line of the same page; at the reading, out-of-nowhere faults per 100 turn lines do not rise.
4. **No one can see it.** Sizing check: the witness knows at least 30% of the new acts (secret acts 8% today, promises 3–6%).
5. **The town turns soap.** Sizing check, on against off: adults exposed or in an affair stay under 10% a decade; reversals per life stay at or under 4 at the 90th percentile (item 1's counter); the share of weighty events repeating a kind (32%) does not rise. These are stops, not targets: a mechanism's parameters come from the state it reads and are never tuned toward them.
