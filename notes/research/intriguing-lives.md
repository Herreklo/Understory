# What makes a life intriguing

_Research, 22 October 2026, agreed by Øystein after PR #85. What makes a simulated life one a reader wants to follow, which kinds of situation make stories, and which belong to the engine. Exit points 5, 2 and 1. Evidence, with every source, in `sources/intrigue-*.md`: our readings re-analysed (`-readings`), games and emergent narrative (`-games`), the psychology (`-science`), and situation kinds audited on 20 throwaway worlds (`-situations`). Scripts in `sources/intrigue-scripts/`._

**The guard, a hard rule.** Storiness must not become scripting. No decision may read a story measure; patterns are found only afterwards. Every proposal below names the state in people that produces it.

## What our readings say

The 14 readings on file give 276 pairs and 552 pages, z-scored within each reading so rounds 29–41 and M38 compare by rank.

- **Kinds of thing decide most of the score.** Lines and kinds explain R² 0.44. Eighteen story features coded from the page (a cast, a feud, a want with a setback, a secret, a reversal, an open end, callbacks, a choice against the grain) add 0.03 together.
- **One feature holds in every test: a turn in the person's own life**: a new job, a partner gained or lost, a birth, a move. Within pairs, with lines, kinds and the arm held, it adds **+0.59 z [+0.31, +0.86]**, worth three to seven kinds. In size-matched pairs where one version read clearly better, only the winner had a turn 8 times, only the loser once.
- **Readers score down sameness and things from nowhere, not errors.** A repeat fault costs −0.25 z, an "out of nowhere" −0.17. Unfit reasons, false lines and wording are marked but cost about nothing.
- **Praise goes to rare acts that cost something.** Losing out, a job turn, being let down and a partner turn are quoted as good reasons at 2 to 2.4 times the average rate; band routine and the festival almost never.
- **The best lives on file** (Oliver Sayer, round 40; Arthur, round 31; Greta, round 41) share a shape: one named rival or partner across the decade, and a want that lands late as a turn and costs something at once. Greta wanted the skipper's job for 18 years, got it, froze at the first meeting, and her rival confronted her in front of everyone. Arthur's and Greta's other versions, the same size, scored about a point lower.
- **What it cannot see.** The busiest adults' pages almost all have a cast and a conflict (98–99%), so neither can be tested. Secrets, open ends and reversals show nothing at this n. All readers are one model.

## What others did, and where it went wrong

- **Talk of the Town:** beliefs that spread and mutate, about hair colour. Knowledge with nothing at stake made no intrigue. Ryan: raw simulation "will almost always lack story structure", and an act chosen by many weighted factors has a cause too diffuse to tell.
- **Crusader Kings:** rivals become nemeses; secrets and hooks are held and used later. Its events recycle within two decades.
- **Dwarf Fortress, The Sims:** the story is picked out of a noisy log by the player, or authored by them.
- **Versu** let only the player push a character against its preference, so no one else changed. **PsychSim** explains well, with goal weights fitted by hand. **Nemesis** captains are promoted _because_ they killed you: a named cause is what players retell.
- **Directors are the counter-example:** RimWorld's storytellers and Left 4 Dead's director make pacing players love, pacing that belongs to no one. Without one we accept lulls and let the page choose.
- **Coded retellings** (40, plus a Prom Week ablation with n = 2): up to three reversals read best. With random choices in place of social reasoning, reversals rose and coherence fell. More events is not more story.

## The science, used

- **Cause and consequence make an event count.** Events on a causal chain are judged more important and recalled better, rising with the number of links; dead ends drop out (replicated since 1985).
- **Interest needs the new and the understandable together** (Silvia), and is unrelated to pleasantness: a grim life can grip.
- **A breach in a small dose:** about one per folk tale is optimal. A rare event needs a chain of ordinary causes behind it, or it reads as false (Labov).
- **Surprise satisfies when its cause is already on the page.** "Recognised afterwards" means the reader can promote an earlier line to the explanation. Suspense needs a want, a person followed and long odds.
- **Weak spots.** No study measures what makes another person's ordinary life intriguing. McAdams' redemption and contamination concern the teller's well-being, and the redemptive self is an American script: a shape to allow, not a target.

## Which situations make stories

Fifteen kinds recur across Polti, Tobias, Booker, Propp, Labov, the sifting patterns and the games. **All fifteen are the engine's:** they need people, ties, wants, beliefs and time. A domain supplies the thing contested and, mostly, a public scoreboard and calendar of stakes, which a setting may declare and never paces.

What recurs across the sources are **triads**: two people and a third term (a good, a person, a secret, a debt) whose claims cannot all be met. The engine's situations are mostly pairs, settled within the month. The audit:

- **Linked:** succession (78–80% lead to a turn, 98% known), death (62%, reaching 4.1 people), making up (79%).
- **Upkeep:** promises, 100 a world-decade, lead somewhere 7% of the time, 3% known. Owning up happens 27 times a decade and leads nowhere, ever.
- **Unlinked:** 56% of blows are followed by a rise within three years; 3% of those rises descend from the blow. The want records no cause, so the record may be losing the chain. Check first.
- **Missing, with the state already there:** sides (39 rifts in five years with friends of both, and no third person ever chooses); secrets used by others (12.7% of adult-months carry one, and only the holder decides about it); debts from real acts (`p.owes` is never read); a third person (no triangle, affair or jealousy in the source).

## For this engine, a life is intriguing when it has

1. **Linked turns:** a turn in the person's own life, with a cause the page already showed and a consequence after it. Today two thirds of knowable turns follow nothing (`reports/density-22oct.md`).
2. **A third term held across time:** a few named others recur because something stands between them.
3. **Rare and costly over routine** on the page; routine folds (`language.md`).
4. **Surprise from the person's own draw,** explained by an earlier line, and a few reversals never counted in order to make them.

## Rounds this could become

Not slotted: the planning step decides what enters "Next", in what order and how each is judged, with Øystein.

1. **Neutral:** a story measure in the battery (life turns per page, the share with a cited cause, counterparts in 3+ weighty events, reversals per life); a dependency test that fails if any engine system imports it, making the guard mechanical; and, if the check confirms it, the want records the event it came from.
2. **Taking sides:** after a rivalry, split or betrayal, a friend of both sides with one, the other or neither, weighed by liking, regard, fairness and whose account they heard first. The side not taken loses liking and trust. Cheapest, and it gives friendship reach (0% today). Close-ties arc.
3. **What is owed, called in:** feelings part one's resentment and gratitude, not a second mechanism. `owes` is set by real costly help or an unrepaired wrong and weighed when the creditor next needs the debtor, so revenge waits for its opening. It replaces the random promise.
4. **A secret others know:** a knower meeting the owner in a hostile or contested situation may bring it out, through the path `caught` uses. The accounts arc's ground.
5. **A third person:** attraction that persists across a partnership, and a choice to act on it. It feeds 4 and 2. Under Rule 8 the obstacle is a tie and an act, never an identity.

**Estimate.** The reading will barely see these. A turn is worth about +0.6 z on a page that gains one, but 73% of busy pages already have one. Giving a linked turn to a fifth of the rest moves the mean pair about +0.03 z, about 0.01 of a raw point, far inside the reading's ±0.35. Each round is kept on its own counter (acts that lead somewhere, decisions flipped, what the witness knows, the linked-turn share) and read for "reads no worse", with random packs as well as the busiest.

## This fails if …

1. **Drama rises by adding events, not links.** Sizing check: the share of weighty events cited by a later one (22% today) must rise. Stop if it does not.
2. **The new situations are upkeep.** Sizing check: the share of side-taking, calling-in or exposure decisions that end or start a tie, or are cited by a weighty event, within three years; stop below 15% (the hat: 2%). Stop too if removing the new state flips under 5% of the decisions it is weighed in.
3. **Turns arrive from nowhere.** Sizing check: at least 70% of new turns have a cause on an earlier line of the same page; at the reading, out-of-nowhere faults per 100 turn lines do not rise.
4. **No one can see it.** Sizing check: the witness knows at least 30% of the new acts (secret acts 8% today, promises 3%).
5. **The town turns soap.** Sizing check, on against off: adults exposed or in an affair stay under 10% a decade; reversals per life stay at or under 4 at the 90th percentile; the share of weighty events repeating a kind (32%) does not rise.
