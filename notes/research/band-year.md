# The band year

_Research for P6 (`notes/build-plan.md` section 4; exits 9, 1, 8), 28 October 2026, on `main` at `b826dec` (round 46 merged off, every system on). Throwaway worlds 9001–9040, run two ways: the usual 30-year past with ten present years, and the whole record from the town's founding (no past, 40 years), so that a band's founders are known. Scripts and output: `notes/research/sources/band-scripts/` (`bands.ts`, `turnover.ts`, `analyse.py`, `analyse-turnover.py`, `analyse-out.txt`). Figures are a mean per world with its 95% interval between worlds, or pooled where said. No engine file was changed, no fresh world was run and nothing was read._

## In short

- **The engine has one kind of group, and it is only its members.** A band (`src/group.ts`, `SETTING.groupKinds`) keeps a name, a founder, works, a standing that decays at each gig, its rows by member, and a chemistry recomputed every month from whoever is in it. Nothing it holds is about how things are done in it. Workplaces have a lead and staff and no state of their own. The choir, the cards and the Anchor regulars are routines with 14–21 holders each and no identity. Households are not groups yet (#59).
- **Bands are common and nearly immortal.** A world has 3.9 ±0.5 bands across 40 years and 3.5 alive at the end. 16.5% ±2.1 of adult-months are spent in one. Only 17 of 157 ended. A band that loses players recruits: 147 of 157 took someone in.
- **Outliving the founders already happens, with nothing carried.** From founding, 23 of 164 bands (0.57 ±0.22 a world) lost every founding member within 40 years and went on. The median band takes about ten years to lose its last founder. In the present decade alone it is 3 of 145. Workplaces turn over far more: 95% lose all their original staff within 40 years, and 12% within one decade. Today nothing of a group's make-up survives that turnover: across 341 workplaces, the members' mean facets after the old guard has gone correlate with the old guard's at −0.12 to +0.10. Recruitment does not select for a character.
- **The row ladder has almost nothing to climb.** `escalation()` is one rule for every band: a second row in three years earns a word, a third gets you asked to leave. A row comes 0.047 times a band-year. In the present decade a world has 1.6 rows, 0.75 words and 0.57 askings-to-leave, and 89 of 157 bands never had a row. A band's character cannot live in its rows, because most bands never have one.
- **Credit is where a band decides most.** Members make 1.7 decisions a band-year that involve another member. The commonest is authorship, the answer to a bandmate's song (cheer it, back it, resent it, claim it), at 0.64 a band-year. Among the band's own matters, recruitment comes next: a band takes in 1.75 newcomers a decade.
- **Estimate: the founders' norm, held by the members after the last founder has gone, correlates with the founders' at about 0.34 (0.2–0.5), against a stop under 0.25.** It clears the stop only if newcomers are told how things are done. Learned from watching acts alone, it would land near 0.1.
- **S1 no longer gates P6.** S1 was in the row because the band was the slice. The front door already reads groups (`groups()` in `boundary.ts`), and `invite.ts` starts and joins bands through it. Playing a band now happens in Overstory's build (decision 24).

## What others did, and where it went wrong

| ----------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **Talk of the Town**          | Businesses with owners who hire through their relationships, families, and a simulated history of more than a century. People remember who worked where.                                                                   | Groups are job slots and kin. No business has a way of doing things, so nothing about it outlives its people except the building.                                              |
| **Crusader Kings III**        | Dynasties and houses last centuries. Legacies are bought with renown. Cultures carry an ethos and traditions, and diverge or hybridise when a ruler chooses. Vassals join factions above a threshold of opinion.           | The character sits on the group as modifiers that the player picks, never in what members believe. Factions are thresholds, and players learn them and game them.              |
| **Dwarf Fortress**            | Civilisations have ethics and values set at world generation, and members' values draw on them. Also positions with succession, religions, performance troupes, mercenary companies, and (v50) guilds that ask for a hall. | The entity holds the values: a mind of its own, the thing exit 9 rules out. | **The Sims 4 (Get Together)** | Clubs with rules the player writes (activities encouraged or discouraged), a leader, and points for following the rules.                                                                                                   | The rules are the player's checklist and a currency. | **RimWorld (Ideology)**       | Each pawn holds an ideoligion with a certainty that conversion wears down. Precepts give mood when kept or broken.                                                                                                         | The content is fixed by the player at the start. A pawn's belief is one number, and compliance is a mood modifier, never a reason in a decision.                               |
| **Football Manager**          | A dressing-room hierarchy, team leaders and a board's club vision. In mentoring groups, senior players pass hidden personality attributes (professionalism, determination) to young ones.                                  | This is the one transmission through members, and it is opaque. Players learn it as a recipe ("put the wonderkid with model professionals") and see only the attribute change. |

The lesson: groups either hold their character themselves, as modifiers or values on the entity (Crusader Kings, Dwarf Fortress), or have it written by the player (The Sims, RimWorld). Football Manager's mentoring is the nearest to a norm carried in members, and its fault is that nobody can see why it worked. Versu's social practices are authored norms inside a scene. None of these games has a group whose character comes from its own early history and passes from member to member.

## What the science says

- **Arbitrary norms persist across full replacement, and decay.** Jacobs & Campbell (1961) planted a norm (confederates who overestimated a light's movement) in groups of two to four. Members were then replaced one at a time. The norm survived the confederates' departure and decayed to the naive level within about five generations. Decay to the naive level within about five generations means that roughly half to two thirds of the excess survives each generation. More arbitrary norms decay faster (MacNeil & Sherif 1976), and norms that do not fit the task are dropped quickly (Weick & Gilfillan 1971). Traditions in laboratory microsocieties with turnover persist in the same way (Baum, Richerson, Efferson & Paciotti 2004). This is the ceiling: a perceptual judgement, said aloud, is as easy to transmit as a norm gets.
- **Norms come from primacy and critical events.** Group norms form in the first interactions, from the scripts members bring (Bettenhausen & Murnighan 1985). Feldman (1984) names four sources: a leader's explicit statement, a critical event in the group's history, the first pattern of behaviour, and what members carry over from other groups. A first meeting's framework lasts until the project's midpoint (Gersick 1988). So a band's credit norm should come from how its first songs were answered, not from its members' average traits.
- **Socialisation is what carries it.** Groups recruit, socialise, maintain and remember members (Moreland & Levine 1982). In meta-analysis, institutional socialisation tactics improve newcomers' social acceptance and role clarity by moderate amounts (Bauer, Bodner, Erdogan, Truxillo & Tucker 2007). In Harrison & Carroll's (1991) model of cultural transmission, how intensely newcomers are enculturated matters more for a culture's stability than how fast people leave.
- **Founders imprint, and changing the blueprint is costly.** Organisations keep the shape of their founding (Stinchcombe 1965). In about 200 young technology firms, changing the founders' employment blueprint went with higher turnover (Baron, Hannan & Burton 2001).
- **Selection alone is weak.** Organisations differ in the modal personality of their members, but the share of variance is small (Schneider 1987; Schneider, Smith, Taylor & Fleenor 1998). This fits the engine's r ≈ 0 for workplaces: carriage has to come from beliefs, not from recruiting alike.

## What a design would need

- **A norm is a belief, held by each member,** about how something is done in this group. It fits the existing store (`w.beliefs`, holder → "subject|key"), with the group as the subject. Each member's belief forms from what they see in the group (authorship answers, who was taken in, how a row ended) and from what they are told. There is no `g.norm` field that decides anything. What the group is like is what its members believe, and that can differ between members.
- **It acts only as a consideration in decisions that already exist:** authorship, `groupstrain`, recruitment and, at workplaces, `criticised` and `asked`. It adds no event per member, so cast size is the band (mean 3.5, at most 4). When it decides, it is the reason, worded with the group's name.
- **Each group's own escalation:** the word and the asking-to-leave become decisions by a member, weighing that member's belief about how rows go here, in place of `escalation()`'s fixed ladder. With rows this rare, this is the second half of the mechanism, not its counter.
- **Telling a newcomer** is the step the estimate depends on. Each incumbent decides, from their own state (how strongly they hold the norm, their boldness, how they like the newcomer), whether to say how things are done here. It goes through `telling.ts`, as an account with its distortion. No count or quota chooses the teller. About 6 newcomers join bands per world-decade, so this is a few lines a decade.

## Estimate

The counter is the founders' imprint. Across bands that have lost every founding member, it is the correlation between the norm as the founders held it, in their last year together, and as the members hold it a year after the last founder has gone. Today it is 0, because no norm exists, and selection carries nothing (workplace facets r ≈ 0, n = 341). Beside it, as a gate, the norm must flip at least 5% of the decisions it is weighed in (P1's bar), or it is words.

Full founder turnover in a band spans about one to one and a half generations: about three newcomers by the time the last founder goes, joining at staggered times. With the laboratory's retention of 0.5–0.7 a generation, the correlation would be about 0.45–0.6. Newcomers' own acts pull against the norm wherever their traits disagree with it, and MacNeil & Sherif's arbitrary norms decay faster, so I put the raw value at 0.45 (0.3–0.6). **Shrunk by a quarter, as a pre-registration must, that is about 0.34 (0.2–0.5). The stop is under 0.25,** the point at which only 58% of bands would still lean the founders' way. The estimate clears the stop on its centre, and its interval spans the stop. Without telling, carriage runs only through acts, in which the norm is one consideration among traits. That gives about 0.1 and fails.

**Power.** From founding, 0.57 bands a world lose every founder within 40 years. 160 throwaway worlds give about 90 such bands and an interval of about ±0.18 on r: enough to tell 0.34 from today's 0, not enough to tell it from 0.25. A 40-year world runs in about 4 seconds, so this is cheap. Adding workplaces (8.5 a world) would give power to spare, but it is a second mechanism, and a second round in the arc.

## This fails if …

1. **It is words.** Sizing check: removing the norm consideration flips at least 5% of the decisions it is weighed in (`flip.ts`). Report authorship separately, since it carries most of them.
2. **The character dies with its founders** (the counter). Sizing check: on 160 worlds from founding, the founders' norm against the members' a year after the last founder has gone, r with its interval. Stop under 0.25. Report the facet-profile r beside it, to show the effect is not selection, and report the variant with telling off.
3. **It is only the members' traits.** Sizing check: the members' mean held norm against their mean on the norm's facet (modesty and sincerity for credit; forgiveness and patience for rows). If r is above 0.7, the norm is a relabelled trait average, not the group's history. Bands formed with the same make-up must differ in their norm by their first events, in at least a third of pairs matched on facets.
4. **It brings big casts or repeats** (round 46). Sizing check: every norm-weighed decision involves only the band's members. Tellers per newcomer are reported, with the median at most 2, and come from state, never a cap. Norm reasons and told lines take no top-25 place in any world. The copy share's one-sided bound stays under +0.6. The commonest band line today ("brought a song … and it did not come to much", 6.3 a world-decade) must not rise. Choir, cards and the Anchor (14–21 holders) stay out of P6.
5. **No one can see it.** Sizing check: the witness knows at least 30% of the acts a norm decided. Today the witness is in a band 2.4 ±1.2 of ten years, and in or close to one (friend, partner, kin) 6.7 ±1.3, with 8 of 40 never. Exit 9's clause, saying what four of five bands are like in one sentence, is answered by model readers from members' pages between rounds. If a game is to read a band's character, that is a read of what members believe, by viewer (S2's reads), announced on #60 before it lands.

## What else the data says

- **Band lines are 2.7% ±0.4 of listed lines,** 56 a world-decade. They are already repetitive. The top four are authorship and the song itself: "did not come to much" (251 over 40 worlds), "it was good" (153), "played it the way it was written" (135), "would not play it the way it was written" (112).
- **Bands almost never end.** A band ends when its chemistry stays low or it has made nothing for two years, and both are rare. This is not P6's to fix, but a design should know that a band's history runs for decades.
- **Overstory has named sports clubs as a kind of group** (#60). That is setting content (`groupKinds`). The mechanism here is the same for any kind of group.
