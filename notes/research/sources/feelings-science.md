# Kinds of feeling

## 1. Appraisal: what decides which feeling

The theories ask much the same questions:

| Question | OCC | Scherer | Engine field |
|---|---|---|---|
| Does it matter to me? | desirability | goal relevance | weight of the want or value |
| Who caused it? | self or other agent | causal attribution | the event's actor |
| Done, or pending? | actual vs prospect | outcome probability | settled or open |
| Can it be changed? | — | coping potential | repairable, reversible, irrevocable |
| Does it break a standard? | praise/blameworthy | normative significance | own values; who knows |
| Whose fortune? | fortunes of others | — | another's gain or loss |

Scherer runs these as ordered checks [4]; Roseman's dimensions are the same [6]. **OCC** gives 22 types from three branches (events, agents' actions, objects) plus compounds: anger = reproach + distress, gratitude = admiration + joy [1]. **Steunebrink, Dastani and Meyer** recast it as an inheritance tree, each type its parent plus one condition, and move relief, satisfaction, disappointment and fears-confirmed under joy/distress: what a hope or fear *becomes* when the outcome arrives [2], so model them as conversions. Ortony's own reduction keeps twelve types [3]. Naming trap: OCC's "resentment" is envy, not a grudge. **Lazarus** gives each emotion one theme: anger, a demeaning offence; sadness, irrevocable loss [5].

The links are probabilistic: no appraisal is necessary or sufficient for anger [8]; appraisal and action readiness discriminate emotions partly independently [7]. Map appraisals to kinds by weights that personality moves.

## 2. Action tendencies

- **Four act families** (Roseman): contact (joy, hope, pride, relief), distance (distress, fear, sadness, regret), attack (anger, guilt), reject (contempt, disgust, shame) [6].
- **Shame and guilt.** Shame goes with hiding, withdrawal and externalised anger, guilt with apology and repair [10]; shame-proneness predicts aggression, displaced too [11]. But across 90 samples (N=12,364) reparability does most of the work: shame links to constructive approach when the failure is reparable (g=.47), away from it when not (g=−.34); reparable, shame and guilt look alike (g=.44, .43) [12].
- **Anger.** 59% of everyday anger follows an act seen as voluntary and unjustified, 28% an avoidable accident; targets reported the relationship strengthened in 48%, weakened in 35% [13]. Anger is short-term attack, long-term reconciliation; contempt is exclusion at both horizons, growing from repeated anger where intimacy and control are low [14].
- **Fear and anger on risk:** fear risk-averse, anger risk-seeking [15]; a 2024 registered replication (N=780) gave mixed support [16].
- **Envy.** Benign envy moves up, malicious pulls the other down [17]; deservingness and control decide which, not how strong [18].
- **Pride:** authentic (effort) or hubristic (self) [20]; raises perseverance on related costly tasks [21].
- **Gratitude.** r=.374 with prosocial behaviour across 91 studies, largest for repaying the benefactor [22]; raises costly helping [23]; predicted the relationship a month later [24].
- **Regret** (own choice) pulls to undo and switch; **disappointment** (no own agency) to give up [25, 26].
- **Grief** oscillates between loss (yearning, withdrawal) and restoration (new routines and roles) [28].
- **Brake.** Emotion shapes behaviour mostly through reflection and *anticipated* feeling [29]: anticipated guilt, regret and shame belong in choices.

## 3. Duration and course

Recall study (233 students, 27 emotions): median episode 2–5 h; sadness longest (about 120 h), shame shortest (about 30 min); guilt outlasts shame, anxiety outlasts fear. Event importance (r≈.58) and rumination (r up to .56) explained 43% of the differences [30]. The stimulus returning, in fact or thought, prolongs it [31].

No episode lasts a month. What lasts is a standing appraisal that keeps re-firing (cause present, person met again, rumination), Frijda's "sentiments" [32]: monthly kinds persist by renewal, not a timer.

Month-scale data:
- **Bereavement** (205 widowed, pre-loss to 18 months): resilient 45.9%, common grief 10.7%, chronic grief 15.6%, chronic depression 7.8%, depressed-improved 10.2%, delayed 3.9%; chronic grief went with dependency and sudden loss [33]. Across adversities: resilience 65.7%, recovery 20.8%, chronic 10.6%, delayed 8.9% [34]. Prolonged grief 9.8% [35]. Ruminators were more depressed at 6 months [36]; strong early reactors stayed below baseline for years [37].
- **Grudges** fade logarithmically, fast then a long tail: +2 log-odds of forgiveness by three months, faster where the relationship keeps value [38]. Love and anger at an ex delay recovery [39].
- **Regret:** actions are regretted more in the short run, inactions in the long run [27].

## 4. Regulation

Gross's families: situation selection and modification, attention, reappraisal, response modulation [40]. Across 306 comparisons: distraction d=.27; dwelling on the feeling d=−.26; reappraising the situation d=.36; perspective-taking d=.45; suppressing expression d=.32 but the feeling d=−.04 [41]. Habitual suppression goes with worse relationships, reappraisal with better [43]; rumination is the strategy most tied to psychopathology [42].

**Sharing:** 80–95% of emotional episodes are told, 58% the same day, 5–6 times, mostly to close others; shame and guilt less [44]. 66% of listeners retell it, 79% when moved [45]. Sharing does not speed recovery [46]. In the engine, feelings are news.

EMA's coping menu: act, plan, seek help, reinterpret, accept, deny, shift blame, disengage [48].

## 5. Mood and emotion in computational models

- **ALMA:** emotions pull a PAD mood; personality sets the default it returns to [47].
- **EMA:** mood is a squashed sum of current emotions and biases which takes focus [48].
- **FAtiMA:** personality is a threshold and a decay rate per emotion type [49].
- **WASABI:** emotion and mood as coupled dynamics, mood the slower [50].
- **GAMYGDALA,** for games: intensity = |goal utility × change in likelihood|; social emotions scale with liking; anger and gratitude are stored on the relation to their cause [51].

Lesson: a feeling has an object; mood is derived and has none. Understory has mood; it lacks object and kind.

## 6. Specification

Each feeling holds kind, cause, object (person, prospect or self), intensity, and whether the cause still stands. Monthly it fades at its kind's rate unless renewed (cause present, object met, a rumination roll). Pulls add to act families and become the decision's reason. Personality sets per-kind thresholds, fade rates and regulation style.

**1. Grief.** *Trigger:* an irrevocable loss of someone or something important (death, breakup, job, home); another's blame adds resentment, one's own guilt. *Pull:* withdraw from new ventures and company (strong); seek a few close people (medium); keep and revisit what was lost (medium); later, restoration. *Course:* trajectory drawn at onset: about half light (1–3 months), 10–20% recover over 6–18, 10–15% chronic past 18, about 4% delayed [33, 34]. *Handling:* rumination lengthens, restoration shortens; dependence and sudden loss raise chronic odds. *Sign:* keeps his wife's coat on the hook and has stopped going to darts.

**2. Worry.** *Trigger:* a pending, uncertain, bad outcome to something important (layoff, debt, illness). Converts on resolution to relief, grief or regret. *Pull:* defer big choices, avoid risk, save, seek reassurance (medium). *Course:* while the prospect is open; no month data. *Handling:* acting on the problem ends it, rumination feeds it; anxiety lowers the threshold. *Sign:* stopped buying rounds and asks the foreman weekly about layoffs.

**3. Resentment (grudge).** *Trigger:* a named person harmed me or mine, by intent or carelessness, unfairly by my standards, unrepaired. *Pull:* avoid them (strong); speak ill (medium); confront (medium, often repairs); retaliate or withhold help (weak; strong at low forgiveness). Repeated, it hardens into contempt. *Course:* mostly gone in 2–3 months, long tail [38]; renewed on meeting. *Handling:* apology and the bond's value speed it, rumination slows it. *Sign:* crosses the street rather than nod to Tom, and tells anyone about the fence.

**4. Guilt.** *Trigger:* my act or omission harmed someone, against my own standard; the act is at fault, not the self; repair possible. *Pull:* apologise, confess, compensate (strong). *Course:* ends when amends are accepted; otherwise 1–3 months, longer if the wronged is gone. *Handling:* repair, confession; honesty-humility raises proneness. *Sign:* has fixed Ann's gutter twice since the row, unasked.

**5. Shame.** *Trigger:* a failure or misdeed read as a flaw of the self, known to others (or a relative's disgrace). *Pull:* if unrepairable, hide, avoid those who know, quit or leave, lash out at witnesses (strong); if repairable, restore standing (medium). *Course:* episodes are short; it re-fires on meeting those who know, while the story circulates. *Handling:* secrecy; blaming the act, not the self, turns it toward guilt. *Sign:* has not been to chapel since the bankruptcy was in the paper.

**6. Envy.** *Trigger:* a comparable person gains what I want, in a domain central to me, and stays in view; deserved and within reach: benign; undeserved: malicious. *Pull:* benign, strive and emulate (medium); malicious, run down, withhold, enjoy their setbacks (medium). *Course:* while the comparison is visible; no month data. *Handling:* avoid the comparison, reappraise deservingness. *Sign:* on the early shift since Jack made foreman, and calls him the owner's nephew.

**7. Pride.** *Trigger:* my own act achieved something valued, credited by me and ideally others; authentic if from effort, hubristic if from self. *Pull:* persevere and take on more in that domain (medium); tell people (medium); hubristic, look down on others (weak). *Course:* about a month. *Handling:* modesty restrains display. *Sign:* volunteered to run the fête again and mentions the prize twice an evening.

**8. Gratitude.** *Trigger:* another's intentional, costly act helped me, not owed or self-serving. *Pull:* repay the benefactor (strong); help others (weak); warmer bond. *Course:* until repaid, or 1–2 months; leaves a lasting bond gain [24]. *Handling:* from a disliked giver it is felt as debt and avoided. *Sign:* has had the Patels to dinner monthly since they took his children in.

**9. Regret.** *Trigger:* my own choice or inaction led to worse than a visible alternative; nobody else wronged (else guilt). *Pull:* undo or switch: reapply, go back, reconcile (medium); avoid similar choices. *Course:* action regrets fade within months; inaction regrets grow over years and surface at milestones [27]. *Handling:* undo, justify, reappraise. *Sign:* has rung the city firm three times about the job he turned down.

## 7. Leave out at a monthly scale

- **Too brief to cross a tick:** fright, surprise, disgust, embarrassment, irritation, amusement, boredom [30]; mood or wording at most.
- **Endings, not states:** relief, satisfaction, disappointment, fears-confirmed (section 1).
- **Owned elsewhere:** love, hatred, contempt (bonds); loneliness (needs); joy and diffuse sadness (mood); hope (likelihood on a want or plan); pity (through bonds); gloating (resentment watching its target fall).
- **Later, if couples need it:** jealousy, as worry about a bond plus resentment of a named rival.

## 8. Where the science is weak

- Kinds are design categories, not natural kinds: no consistent brain signature per category [9]; appraisal links are contingent [8].
- Most data are recalled or lab episodes of minutes to days; only grief, forgiveness and regret have month-scale data. Other months are extrapolation.
- Action tendencies predict acts weakly [29]; fear and anger on risk replicated only in part [16].
- "Shame withdraws, guilt repairs" is overstated; reparability carries most of it [12].
- Benign and malicious envy as two emotions is disputed [19]: one kind, two pulls.
- Trajectory shares vary by population and are Western: starting odds, never targets.
