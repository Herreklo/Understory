# Review of `notes/research/intriguing-lives.md` (Fable 5.1, 22 October 2026)

Reviewed: the note, its four sources, the scripts, and the code it cites. The audit was re-run on seeds 9001–9020 (`dump.ts` then `analyse.py`, output kept in the session scratchpad, not in the repo). Every audit number in the note reproduces exactly (39 rifts, 12.7%, 27.0 owned up at 0%/0%, promises 7%/3%, 3% of rises, 29% of secrets out, 56%/30% blow-rise). Every reading number checked matches `analysis-out.txt`, `themes-out.txt` and `top-out.txt` (+0.59 [+0.31, +0.86] in 49 pairs, R² 0.437→0.466 adjusted, repeat −0.251 and out-of-nowhere −0.170 in rounds 37–41, matched pairs 8/1, 276 pairs/552 pages, turn share 73%). `p.owes` is declared at `src/world.ts:257` and neither written nor read (the `owes` in `src/carry.ts:290` is a local). No affair, triangle or jealousy exists in `src/` (`attraction` is orientation only). The arithmetic is sound. The findings below are about what the numbers are made to say.

## BLOCKS

**B1. "A third term held across time" is presented as a finding of the readings; the readings do not support it.** The note's section "For this engine, a life is intriguing when it has … 2. A third term held across time" sits under a page of reading statistics, and line 16 says only that cast and conflict are saturated. But the testable proxies for a persistent counterpart are not saturated and show nothing or the reverse: `cast_core` (one person in 5+ years, 79% of pages) +0.05 [−0.12, +0.23] across lives, +0.15 [−0.12, +0.40] within pairs; `feud3` (same opponent in 3+ years, ~50%) −0.06 and −0.06; in the 42 matched pairs the loser had `feud3` more often than the winner (3 against 8, net −5) and `cast_core` 3 against 5; `cast3` is +0.23 with proxy kinds but +0.04 with the key's. The claim rests on the games source (Nemesis, CK3 rivals, Boatmurdered's elephants), the story-theory synthesis ("triads"), and three exemplary lives chosen after reading the scores. That is a legitimate hypothesis; it is not what 276 pairs say. Since shortlist items 2–5 are all third-term mechanisms, the planning step will take their expected reading effect from this line. Fix: move (2) under the literature, state that the readings' proxies are null or negative, and say the exemplary lives are post hoc.

**B2. The side-taking trigger rate is given as a total over 20 worlds where every other rate is per world-decade.** "39 rifts in five years with friends of both" is 39 across 20 worlds × 5 years = **3.9 per world-decade** at ≥2 friends of both (7.1 at ≥1; 22.5 if `cooled` is counted as a rift, which the audit table's row 6 does and its closer count does not, so the source contradicts itself). The note calls taking sides "cheapest" and ranks it first; at four firings a world-decade, a 24-pair pack (8 worlds, 3 busiest) would carry roughly 30 side-takings in all, few on the chosen pages, and the sizing's 15% threshold would be judged on about 60 decisions in 20 worlds. The planner needs the same unit as the other rows (per world-decade, with an interval) and the ≥1 and `cooled` variants, or it will mis-rank the mechanisms by how often they could be seen.

## SHOULD

**S1. The turn finding is confounded in a way the note drops.** The source's last caveat (both versions come from different worlds, so a turn marks a life that diverged) does not reach the note. The structure of the result argues for the confound: a _home_ turn, the one kind that is a bare line, is −0.08 [−0.61, +0.42] within pairs; a _birth_ line ("was born to", 0.0 good-reason citations per 100, `themes-out.txt`) is the strongest at +0.43, which can only be the family life around it. Pages with a partner or household turn carry 9.2 conflict lines against 6.8 without and more recurring people (my check on the 552 pages). The M38 null, the only design where a turn differs by chance, has 3 pairs differing on it. So "+0.59 for a turn" is closer to "+0.59 for a life with a job, partner or family arc", and a mechanism that adds a turn line without the arc should not expect it. The note also omits that the turn rule was tightened once after seeing results (+0.47 → +0.59); give both numbers.

**S2. The +0.03 z estimate is for an intervention the shortlist does not propose.** It multiplies the turn effect by a guessed "fifth of the rest" gaining a turn; nothing explains the fifth, and only item 5 (a third person → split or divorce) produces a page-turn. Taking sides, debts and exposure produce `cooled`, hostile acts and `caught`-like events, none a "turn" by the page rule. The conclusion (the reading will not see it; judge on the round's counter and "reads no worse") is right, but for a different reason: the trigger rates in B2. Say the reading effect is unsized and give the per-world firing rates instead.

**S3. The praise pattern is reported from the retired brief only.** The "2 to 2.4 times" figure is 1.8–2.4 (let down 5.6/3.0 = 1.9, partner turn 1.8), and under the current every-fault brief (M38, 96 pages) the pattern is gone: lost out 3.3 against 2.5 for all lines, let down 1.0 (below average), job turn 2.8. Likewise repeat costs −0.11 and out-of-nowhere −0.05 under the current brief, not −0.25 and −0.17. The M38 sample is small; the note should still show both columns, because future readings use the M38 brief. "Costs" is also causal language for a within-reader regression of score on the reader's own fault counts; "goes with" is what the source says.

**S4. "Only the holder decides about [a secret]" is false.** `src/effects.ts:290–322` runs a `discovery` situation in which a confidant chooses `keepsecret`, `tell` or `confrontsecret`; the audit itself counts it at 3.1 a world-decade (68% lead somewhere, 6% known). Item 4 extends an existing path (to knowers who are not confidants, in hostile situations) rather than filling a gap; the note should say so, and the sizing should count the existing path as the baseline.

**S5. Fail-ifs 3 and 5 name measures that do not exist yet, and fail-if 1 cannot fail.** (1) "The share of weighty events cited by a later one must rise": any new act that cites its trigger raises this by construction, so it tests only that the builder wrote a citation; fail-if 2 (do the new acts themselves lead anywhere) is the real check, and (1) should either go or get a margin over the 20-world interval. (3) "70% of new turns have a cause on an earlier line of the same page" needs a page-level counter that does not exist and does not say which "turn" (the page rule or `TURNS`). (5) "reversals per life ≤ 4 at the 90th percentile" needs the reversal counter proposed as item 1; so item 1 must precede every other round, which the note should state as an ordering constraint. Fail-if 2's 15% threshold on ~60 decisions (B2) has an interval of about ±9 points; say so.

**S6. Missing for the planning step.** Per mechanism: the exit-criterion point it moves (the header names 5, 2 and 1; no item is mapped); the trigger rate per world-decade and how many of a pack's 24 pages it would touch; its dependencies (5 feeds 3 and 2; 3 and 5 need 1's measure); and what "replaces the random promise" means for the identity check (removal of promises must sit behind the switch). The audit's output file is not in the repo (`analyse-out.txt` is missing while the readings' outputs are kept); add it, and append the run to `docs/seeds.md`, which the header asks for even for diag ranges.

## NIT

- Promises: the note's 7%/3% are the kept promise's figures; broken ones lead 12%/known 6%. Give the pooled numbers.
- Owning up at 0%/0% is structural: `ownup` has w 1.35 (weighty) but nothing in the engine ever cites an `ownup` (0 of 432 events across 20 worlds). The interpretation "amends never do" is fine; say the cause is that no situation reads it.
- "Worth three to seven kinds": at the within-pair kind rate (+0.106 per kind) the interval is 3–8 kinds; at the across-lives rate (+0.08) 4–11. Either is fine; name the rate used.
- "About 0.01 of a raw point" is on the M38 scale (2.4 z per point); on the old brief it is 0.02.
- Line 15 "scored about a point lower": Arthur 1.0, Greta 1.7 points.
- `secret_any` reads slightly worse (−0.22 within pairs; −0.58 in the M38 re-read). The note notes it in the source but the main note's item 4 (a secret others know) does not weigh that the secret lines on file are among the most formulaic; exposure would add more of them unless the words change.
- The three "best lives" all include a marriage under strain, and all three were read under the old brief with three readers. Post hoc, as B1 says.

## The guard

No proposal lets a decision read a story measure. Taking sides weighs liking, regard, fairness and accounts; debts weigh an event; exposure weighs honesty-humility and the bond; attraction grows from liking, chemistry, distance and doubts. The dependency test (no engine import of the sifter or the measure) is necessary but not sufficient: a system could recompute "reversals so far" from `p.events` without importing anything. The round review should also check that no decision reads a count of its own kind of event for pacing. Fail-if 5's caps (10% exposed or in an affair, p90 reversals ≤ 4) are legitimate as sizing stops, but if a builder tunes rates toward them in the sizing loop that is a dose set by a story measure; the pre-registration should fix the mechanism's parameters from the state it reads, not from the cap.

## Verdict

The numbers are right and reproducible. The note misleads in two places that the planning step will lean on: it lets the readings appear to support the persistent third term (B1), and it gives the one mechanism it ranks first a trigger count in a unit that makes it look twenty times more frequent than it is (B2). Fix both; carry the turn confound and the current-brief numbers (S1–S3); correct the secret path (S4); make the fail-ifs runnable and ordered (S5).
