## BLOCKS

**B1. The trigger arithmetic is loose, and the exposed set is the same people every time.** "About 15 exposed (old, ill, alone and old)" lists a subset twice: `aloneOld` (6.2) is inside `old` (13.1); the union of old and ill is about 15 by luck. "1–2 deciders each" on 3 conditions gives 45–90 decisions, not 60–90. "About 3 conditions" assumes every storm (3.1 ±0.7) becomes one, while the mechanism says a storm "may" cut the power; a sizing at half that rate has half the decisions. The deeper problem: exposure read from age 75+ exposes the same 13 adults at each of a decade's three conditions, with the same one or two ties deciding each time, so the line "who came for her and who did not" is a repeat by construction, which is the festival the note itself warns of (19% of repeat faults). Fix: give the condition rate as a share of storms, the exposed set as a union with its interval over worlds (the audit has it per world), the decision count as a range from that, and make the repeat a stop: the same helper-and-helped pair must not be the commonest condition line (fail-if 4 can carry it).

**B2. Fail-if 2 cannot fail.** Deciders are "each exposed person's closest ties"; the 8% alone and friendless (5 a world) have none, so their helped share is 0, the ratio is 0, and "no exposed person is ever left without help" is impossible, whatever the mechanism does. The check then reports the design, not a result, and the planning step would read "ties decide who is helped" as tested. Fix: draw deciders as `collection.ts` does, from `around(w, x)` (colleagues, circle, friends, and a small chance of anyone near), so a neighbour can reach the friendless; then the ratio is emergent, and the stop (0.7) can be argued from Kaniasty and Norris (unequal from the start) rather than set.

**B3. The help decision's home is not named, and the two situations it would reuse carry cooldowns.** "The exposed ask or manage" is `request.ts`, which refuses a person within 5 months of a `fam:seeking` and anyone asked in the last 24 months (`tried['ask:'+q.id]`); `collection.ts` refuses a person collected for within 24 months. With three conditions a decade on the same people (B1), a reused cooldown would silently gate the second and third rounds of help, and the guard binds every new reader. Fix: say whether help is a new situation (then no cooldown, and `guard.ts` checks it) or runs through `request`/`collection` (then list each cooldown with the one-line justification the guard needs, or say they are cut behind the switch).

## SHOULD

**S1. "Reach should rise a point or two" is unsupported; the conclusion stands for a better reason.** Reach (`src/tools/consequences.ts:56–57`) is the share of weighty events with a weighty descendant naming someone outside the source's `who`. On the re-run dump it is 6.7% of about 1,290 weighty events a world-decade. A condition event whose `who` is the exposed set reaches through its help acts: three new reaching events a decade. The 45–90 help acts themselves, if weighty, name helper and helped and their descendants (owes, friends) stay between those two: non-reaching, and they enlarge the denominator. Net: (86+3)/(1,290+75) ≈ 6.5%, flat to slightly down. So the plan's counter (reach to 15%) would indeed fail a working round, but because it is the wrong measure for this mechanism, not because it moves slowly. Say that, with the arithmetic, and drop the guessed rise.

**S2. Fail-if 1 is not "on against off", and counts the mechanism's own second step.** Off has no help acts, so the comparison is absolute. `follow` counts any weighty descendant: if help sets `owes` and P1's "debts called in" is kept, help → called-in is a chain by design, as rescue → froze is for the accident (100% "leads"). Name the descendant kinds that count (ends or starts a tie, a move, money, a want, a turn) and exclude the condition module's own kinds. State the P1 branch too: if P1 is not kept, `owes` (`world.ts:257`) still has no reader, and "help sets owes" fails the consequence rule.

**S3. Fail-if 3 has no comparison arm and names an object the engine lacks.** "Hostile acts toward the blamed person or body after a blamed condition, against the same window after one with no one to blame": toward whom in the second case? And a "body" (the council, the plant) is not a target of a cooled tie or a hostile act anywhere in `src/`; P2's sides are between people. Define the unblamed arm (acts between the exposed and their deciders, say) and the blamed object as a person until bodies exist.

**S4. The city contract.** Position 4 keeps "the town" as a scope. In a city that is 1,250 neighbourhoods at once; contract §1 pairs people only through bounded settings. Say that scope is a neighbourhood (a town being one, §10's pattern), and that "someone clears it" and "a body" are chosen from a setting. Also carry the games file's lesson 8 (PsychSim: believed risk, which can be wrong, drives the decision) into the design or say why not: exposure read from true state is the one place a player cannot be surprised.

**S5. The brief's items, and hedges dropped between source and note.** Ulrich [19] is in the source with its caveats but not in the note; Kaplan is named nowhere (attention restoration is his). The Klimstra shares (15% / 30%) are from a press summary; another gives 17% / 27% / 9% / 48%; cite the paper's table. The four types are Klimstra's, not "Denissen; Klimstra". Lucas and Lawless's 0.02 is "from a summary" in the source only. Cordova is one case and the source calls the natural/technological split debated; "Blame splits" is a heading, not a finding. Kim 2025 pools SPAQ screens; the latitude link is contested. The heat figure (70 × 0.04 / 6 ≈ 0.5) holds at 1 SD; say so.

**S6. Prior art, two overstatements.** "Players find it all cost" is said of cave adaptation only; Talk of the Town's "no weather or hardship" and CK3's developer line are marked inferred or via search summary in the source and stated flat in the note; Frostpunk's "critics" were some critics.

## NITS

- The storm's 1% is structural: nothing cites a `go` or `hangback` but the wave, as nothing cites an `ownup`. Say so; it is the finding.
- The accident's "100% leads" is by construction (rescue and froze are its descendants); only the rescue/froze rows are evidence. Froze's 50% is mostly `letgo` (35%, "back to normal, as if forgotten"), a thin lead.
- Round 1's hangback fix needs the other deciders' answers, which are not known when the line is logged (instances resolve one by one); say whether the text is written at the storm's end or reworded unconditionally.
- 20% of adults 75+ is an end-of-run snapshot of an old placeholder town; the exposed interval over worlds belongs in the table.
- Fail-if 4's counters are N1a's; the ordering constraint (N1a before P3's sizing) should be stated, as the plan does for P1.

## The guard

No proposal reads a story measure or a count for pacing: the chance is the setting's rate, exposure reads the person's state, help weighs ties and cost, blame is an appraisal; "each month it may clear" is renewal, not a cooldown. The risks are B3 and the sizing loop: with "about 3" and "60–90" written before the mechanism, a builder could tune toward them; fix the rate as a share of storms and let the count fall out.

## Verdict

The audit is right and reproducible, the positions are sound, and the engine-versus-setting split is the best part. The round as proposed cannot be sized honestly until B1–B3 are fixed: the decision count is wrong, the "left out" check cannot fail, and the help decision may inherit pacing it must not have. S1 corrects the reach argument the planning step will quote.
