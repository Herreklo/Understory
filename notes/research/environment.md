# Environment and shared conditions

_Research, 22 October 2026, on Øystein's request (a scary house, the power going out in a street or a town, stunning nature, nice weather). How shared conditions change what people do and how their lives go, for this engine; not atmosphere. Exit points 2 and 1. Evidence, with every source, in `sources/environment-*.md`: the engine audited and counted on 20 throwaway worlds (`-audit`, output in `environment-scripts/analyse-out.txt`), the science (`-science`) and games (`-games`). [A] is the audit, [1–28] the science file's sources and [G1–G32] the games file's._

**The guard holds here as everywhere.** No decision reads a story measure, or a count of its own kind of event for pacing. A condition arises from the world's state and the setting's chance; a director that schedules hardship for drama is out.

## What the engine does today

- **One shared condition, shared in name only.** The storm (`situations/risk.ts`) is one draw a month for the town, 3.1 ±0.7 a world-decade, and each of about 5.5 harbour workers decides alone, about money, whether to go out. Going pays 1.5 savings and hurts 6%; nobody remembers who went or who stayed. Its 17 decisions a world-decade **lead somewhere 1% of the time** (go 2%, hang back 0%), below promises (7–12%) and the hat (2%) [A].
- **The same shape with a person in it links.** The accident at work (`danger.ts`) is also public, dangerous and courage-or-caution, but the choice is whether to go in after a named colleague, who remembers it. Going in leads somewhere 40% of the time (a friendship 29%), freezing 50% [A]. The difference is a remembered act between two people, not the event's size.
- **Weather is portrait content.** 31% of adults carry a weather line (loves, quirks, counts); no decision reads one [A].
- **Two lines are false** (exit point 3). "Hung back when the others went out into the storm" is said in the 8% of storms where nobody went; "proud of the day in the storm" names every rescue, 151 portrait lines, none from a storm (`persona.ts:398`) [A].
- **Nothing hits a set of people at once** except death's reach. A town is one neighbourhood; there are no homes with qualities, no street, no season.
- **Who a hardship would find:** 20% of adults are 75 or over, 36% live alone, 12% have no friend, 8% are both alone and friendless [A].

## What others did, and where it went wrong

- **The Sims 4** has seasons, weather, cold and heat that can kill, and a decor score that becomes a mood bonus. Temperature changes acts (outfits, death); decor reaches acts only through emotions that gate skill gain. Small positive moodlets stack until happiness is the default, so a modder rebuilt it to make happiness rare [G14–G18].
- **RimWorld** splits the physical from the felt. Darkness slows work to ×0.8, and solar flares, cold snaps, toxic fallout and volcanic winter act through power, crops, heat and health; the aurora is the one condition that changes what a pawn chooses (skygazing 8×). Beauty, room impressiveness and weather (soaked −3) only add to one mood bar, and a break's message names the last small thought, so "ate without table" takes the blame for a firefight [G1–G9].
- **Dwarf Fortress** lists rain, miasma, room value and fine furniture as thoughts, and personality sets how much each hurts: dwarves who like the outdoors ignore rain. Acts change only at a breakdown, after stress builds over years, so one day's rain is invisible. Cave adaptation is the exception that acts (sunlight makes soldiers vomit), and players find it all cost [G10–G13].
- **Frostpunk** makes the cold a shared pressure: heat zones, sickness from home and workplace temperature, the homeless worst off, and laws (child labour, 24-hour shifts) the player must pass. Citizens decide nothing; the dilemma is the player's alone, and critics found it hollow because nothing judges it [G19–G21]. **Banished**: winter empties the fields, hungry workers walk off to find food, and towns collapse. Villagers are a workforce with no inner life [G22, G23].
- **Project Zomboid** cuts water and power on a random day in the first month by default. Every plan changes, because the condition bites through stocks and a deadline everyone can see coming [G24].
- **Crusader Kings III**: a county's development and control change tax and levies. Winter, added in patch 1.3, raises army attrition, and a developer said it does nothing outside war. Epidemics kill, reshape successions and offer rulers isolation as a decision, and reviewers found small outbreaks a stream of notifications [G25–G28].
- **Talk of the Town** has places where characters meet, and businesses that open and close move people; no weather or hardship. **Versu** has no physical world. **PsychSim**'s Disaster World simulates residents with beliefs, which may be wrong, through a series of hurricanes; in an earlier domain a resident leaves by weighing the risk they believe falls on them and those close to them. It was built to test social-science inference, not to tell stories [G29–G32].
- **Where it goes wrong, across them:** a condition changes acts only when a decision reads it; a summed mood bar makes the environment decoration and blames the wrong cause; a condition kept inside one system (CK3's winter) stays out of lives; and the hardship that makes a story forces a choice about who bears the cost. None of them gives that choice to the simulated people about each other, which is what the disaster literature says decides the outcome.

## The science, used

- **Shared events mostly bind, along ties that already exist.** People in disasters do not panic; survivors do most of the first rescue and converge rather than flee (Fritz, Quarantelli; the "therapeutic community") [1–3]. Help is unequal from the start and wears off within months (Kaniasty and Norris) [5]; strong networks speed recovery and also exclude (Aldrich) [8].
- **The one left out is the story.** Chicago 1995: not leaving home each day OR 6.7, living alone 2.3; social contacts protected [6]. Two adjacent neighbourhoods under the same heat lost about 40 and 4 per 100,000 [7].
- **Blame splits.** Harm with no one to blame tends to bind; harm with someone to blame made Cordova a "corrosive community" for years after the Exxon Valdez [12].
- **A condition reveals the state it falls on.** New York's 1977 blackout (after dark, in a recession; about 2,000 businesses looted) and 2003 (daylight, calm) were the same outage on a different city [9–11].
- **Turns cluster after.** Marriages, births and divorces all rose for a year in the counties Hurricane Hugo hit [13]. One study, county-level.
- **Places act through use and through lasting cause.** Green shared space made neighbours know each other because they used it (randomly assigned buildings) [15]. Damp homes went with depression at OR about 1.4, partly through a lower sense of control [16]. A frightening place follows its reputation [20].
- **Restoration and weather are below the tick.** Nature raises positive affect moderately for minutes to hours [17]; attention restoration is ambiguous [18]. Daily weather explains little mood; about half of people are unaffected, and the rest split into types (Denissen; Klimstra) [22, 23]. Weather moves life satisfaction by about 0.02 on a 4-point scale (Lucas and Lawless) [24], and climate not at all, though people expect it to (Schkade and Kahneman) [25]. Heat raises interpersonal violence about 4% per SD [26]; on our town that is under one hostile act a world-decade. Winter-pattern seasonal depression touches about 5%, 9% subsyndromal [28].
- **On a monthly tick,** a day's weather or a view is decoration: lines with no act, the trap the feelings audit found in 41 kinds. What lasts months is a condition that keeps firing (a road closed, the power out, a hard winter, a damp home), which matches the feelings note: a state persists by renewal, not a timer.

## Positions

1. **Shared events and conditions: yes, as shared hardship's trigger.** The lead's prior holds with two corrections. A shared event alone is upkeep (the storm, 1%); it binds or splits only when it creates a decision about a named person that someone remembers (the accident, 40–50%). And the size of the event decides little: ties before it, who is exposed, and whether someone is to blame decide who helps, who is left out and who is blamed. All three are decisions between people the engine's grammar already has (`request`, `collection`, `remember`, `owes`).
2. **Places with qualities: two pieces, with homes (#59).** A home's lasting condition (damp, cold, cramped) as a cause of stress that renews each month and is read by decisions to fix, move or complain, and that sets exposure when a condition hits. Attachment to a place as a weight in the 14 offers to leave a world-decade (8.9 declined, 5.1 taken) and in coming home (4.4) [A]. A scary house is a belief about a place, which the town's talk already carries; it belongs to a game's content. Restoration as mood is out.
3. **Weather and season as mood: no.** Its real sizes are below what a monthly decision can show, and every route to an act runs through a lasting condition (2 or 1). Portrait weather lines stay lines; they must be true.
4. **Engine or setting.** The engine owns a **condition**: a scope (a trade, a workplace, a neighbourhood, the town; a "street" is a neighbourhood in the city), a start and a renewal (each month it may clear; a road stays shut until someone clears it), a cause (nobody, or a named person or body), the need it creates, and exposure read from the person's state (age, illness, living alone, no friend; later the home), with how hard it weighs set by their facets, as Dwarf Fortress lets the outdoor-lover ignore rain. It is public within its scope. The setting owns the kinds (storm, power cut, snow on the road, flood), their monthly rates and season, who is blameable (the council, the plant, a landlord) and the words. The town's rates are a placeholder, never a target.

**Estimate.** From the accident's 40–50% shrunk toward zero, about 20% of help decisions should lead somewhere within three years, against the storm's 1%. A town-wide condition touches nearly every page in its window, so the reading can see it, but only as a new kind of line if each page says who came for that person and who did not; a town line on every page is the festival again (19% of repeat faults). Reach (exit 2: 6% today, 15% needed) should rise a point or two, not to 15% on its own. So the build plan's primary counter for P3 (reach to 15%) would fail a round that works; the counter this note proposes is fail-if 1's link rate, with reach reported beside it. The plan's P3 trigger, a personal blow (0.58 per 100 a month), stays; a condition is a blow to a set of people at once. The Hugo turns are not modelled and are not claimed.

## This fails if …

1. **It is upkeep, like the storm.** Sizing check: `environment-scripts/analyse.py`'s `follow` on the new help and no-help acts, 40 worlds (9001–9040), on against off: stop if under 15% lead somewhere within 36 months.
2. **Ties do not decide who is helped.** Sizing check: the share of exposed adults helped, for those alone and friendless against those with kin or a friend in town; stop if the ratio is above 0.7, or if no exposed person is ever left without help.
3. **It only binds.** Sizing check: on the on arm, cooled ties and hostile acts toward the blamed person or body in the 12 months after a blamed condition, against the same window after one with no one to blame; stop if they do not differ beyond the 20-world interval.
4. **It becomes the festival.** Sizing check, with N1a's counters: on the busiest pack's pages, condition lines' copy share must not exceed the page mean (21%), and kinds of line per page must rise.
5. **No one can see it, or a director creeps in.** Sizing check: the witness knows at least 30% of help acts (the storm's acts 72–77%, the town 23%); and the guard's lint and import test pass on the condition module, whose chance reads only the setting's rate, the season and the world's physical state.
