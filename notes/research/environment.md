# Environment and shared conditions

_Research, 22 October 2026, on Øystein's request (a scary house, a power cut in a street or a town, stunning nature, nice weather): how shared conditions change what people do, not atmosphere. Exit points 2 and 1. Evidence, with every source, in `sources/environment-*.md`: the engine audited and counted on 20 throwaway worlds (`-audit`, output in `environment-scripts/analyse-out.txt`), the science (`-science`) and games (`-games`). [A] is the audit, [1–28] the science file's sources and [G1–G32] the games file's. Reviewed by Fable 5.1 (`sources/review-environment-fable.md`); its three blocking findings are fixed here._

**The guard holds here as everywhere.** No decision reads a story measure, or a count of its own kind of event for pacing. A condition arises from the world's state and the setting's chance; a director that schedules hardship for drama is out.

## What the engine does today

- **One shared condition, shared in name only.** The storm (`situations/risk.ts`) is one draw a month for the town, 3.1 ±0.7 a world-decade, and each of about 5.5 harbour workers decides alone, about money, whether to go out. Going pays 1.5 savings and hurts 6%; nobody remembers who went or who stayed. Its 17 decisions a world-decade **lead somewhere 1% of the time** (go 2%, hang back 0%), below promises (7–12%) and the hat (2%) [A]. That is structural: nothing in the engine cites a `go` or a `hangback` except the wave, as nothing cites owning up.
- **The same shape with a person in it links.** The accident at work (`danger.ts`) is also public, dangerous and courage-or-caution, but the choice is whether to go in after a named colleague, who remembers it. Going in leads somewhere 40% of the time (a friendship 29%), freezing 50%, though mostly to a thin "back to normal" (35%) [A]. The difference is a remembered act between two people, not the event's size.
- **Weather is portrait content.** 31% of adults carry a weather line (loves, quirks, counts); no decision reads one [A].
- **Two lines are false** (exit point 3). "Hung back when the others went out into the storm" is said in the 8% of storms where nobody went; "proud of the day in the storm" names every rescue, 151 portrait lines, none from a storm (`persona.ts:398`) [A].
- **Nothing hits a set of people at once** except death's reach. A town is one neighbourhood; there are no homes with qualities, no street, no season.
- **Who a hardship would find,** at the end of the run in this old placeholder town: 13.8 ±1.2 adults a world are 75 or over or ill (21%; 10–19 across worlds); 36% of adults live alone, 12% have no friend, 8% are both [A].

## What others did, and where it went wrong

- **Mood bars make the environment decoration.** The Sims 4's moodlets stack until happiness is the default [G14–G18]. RimWorld's beauty, rooms and weather only add to one bar, and a break names the last small thought ("ate without table") [G1–G9]. Dwarf Fortress's rain feeds stress that moves over years, so a day's rain is invisible; personality sets how much it hurts (outdoor-lovers ignore rain) [G10–G13].
- **Conditions act when something reads them.** RimWorld's darkness, flares and cold snaps act through work speed, power, crops and health [G2, G5]. Project Zomboid's power and water shutoff changes every plan, through stocks and a deadline [G24]. Crusader Kings III's winter stays inside war (a developer is reported to have said so); its epidemics kill and reshape successions, and small ones become a stream of notifications [G25–G28].
- **The cost falls on the player, never between people.** Frostpunk's cold forces laws on the player; citizens decide nothing, and some critics found the dilemmas hollow [G19–G21]. Banished's hungry workers walk off; villagers have no inner life [G22, G23].
- **The social simulators have no hardship.** None was found in Talk of the Town; Versu has no physical world. PsychSim's Disaster World runs residents with beliefs, which can be wrong, through hurricanes; believed risk drives the decision [G29–G32].
- **So:** none gives the simulated people the choice the disaster literature says decides the outcome: who helps whom.

## The science, used

- **Shared events mostly bind, along ties that already exist.** People in disasters do not panic; survivors do most of the first rescue and converge rather than flee (Fritz, Quarantelli; the "therapeutic community") [1–3]. Help is unequal from the start and wears off within months (Kaniasty and Norris) [5]; strong networks speed recovery and also exclude (Aldrich) [8].
- **The one left out is the story.** Chicago 1995: not leaving home each day OR 6.7, living alone 2.3, social contacts protective [6]; two adjacent neighbourhoods under the same heat lost about 40 and 4 per 100,000 [7].
- **Blame may split.** After the Exxon Valdez, litigation and blame made Cordova a "corrosive community" for years, where harm with no one to blame tends to bind [12]. One case, and the split is debated: used here as an appraisal, not a law.
- **A condition reveals the state it falls on.** New York's 1977 blackout (after dark, in a recession; about 2,000 businesses looted) and 2003 (daylight, calm) were the same outage on a different city [9–11].
- **Turns cluster after.** Marriages, births and divorces all rose for a year in the counties Hurricane Hugo hit [13]. One study, county-level.
- **Places act through use and through lasting cause.** Green shared space made neighbours know each other because they used it (randomly assigned buildings) [15]. Damp homes went with depression at OR about 1.4, partly through a lower sense of control [16]. A frightening place follows its reputation [20].
- **Restoration is short and modest.** Nature raises positive affect moderately for minutes to hours, with large heterogeneity [17]; Kaplan's attention restoration has ambiguous support [18]; Ulrich's stress reduction leans on one hospital's window study, 23 patients against 23 matched, a historical match [19].
- **Weather is small on average.** Daily weather explains little mood, with individual differences the Big Five do not explain (Denissen) [22]; about half of people are unaffected, the rest fall into types such as rain haters (Klimstra; summaries disagree on the shares) [23]. Weather moves life satisfaction about 0.02 on a 4-point scale (Lucas and Lawless, from a summary) [24]; climate not at all, though people expect it to [25]. Heat raises interpersonal violence about 4% per SD in field data, less clearly in the lab [26, 27]: on our town's ~70 hostile acts a world-decade, about 0.5 more. Winter-pattern seasonal depression touches about 5% (9% subsyndromal) in screening surveys [28].
- **On a monthly tick,** a day's weather or a view is decoration: lines with no act, the trap the feelings audit found in 41 kinds. What lasts months is a condition that keeps firing (a road closed, the power out, a damp home): a state persists by renewal, not a timer.

## Positions

1. **Shared events and conditions: yes, as shared hardship's trigger.** The lead's prior holds with two corrections. A shared event alone is upkeep (the storm, 1%); it binds or splits only when it creates a decision about a named person that someone remembers (the accident, 40–50%). And the size of the event decides little: ties before it, who is exposed, and whether someone is to blame decide who helps, who is left out and who is blamed. All three are decisions between people that the grammar can carry, with `remember` and `owes` already there.
2. **Places with qualities: two pieces, with homes (#59).** A home's lasting condition (damp, cold, cramped) as a cause of stress that renews each month and is read by decisions to fix, move or complain, and that sets exposure when a condition hits. Attachment to a place as a weight in the 14 offers to leave a world-decade (8.9 declined, 5.1 taken) and in coming home (4.4) [A]. A scary house is a belief about a place, which the town's talk already carries; it belongs to a game's content. Restoration as mood is out.
3. **Weather and season as mood: no.** Its real sizes are below what a monthly decision can show, and every route to an act runs through a lasting condition (2 or 1). Portrait weather lines stay lines; they must be true.
4. **Engine or setting.** The engine owns a **condition**: a scope that is a bounded setting (a trade, a workplace, a neighbourhood; a town is one neighbourhood and a "street" is one in the city, so a city never runs one over everyone at once), a start and a renewal (each month it may clear; a road stays shut until someone clears it), a cause (nobody, or a named person or body), the need it creates, and exposure read from the person's state (age, illness, living alone, no friend; later the home), with how hard it weighs set by their facets, as Dwarf Fortress lets the outdoor-lover ignore rain. It is public within its scope, but a decider weighs what they know of the exposed person's state (illness is `close` knowledge), so help can miss someone nobody knew was ill: believed risk, as in PsychSim. The setting owns the kinds (storm, power cut, snow on the road, flood), their monthly rates and season, who is blameable (a person holding a post: the council's lead, the plant's manager, a landlord) and who clears what, and the words. The town's rates are a placeholder, never a target.

**Estimate.** From the accident's 40–50% shrunk toward zero, about 20% of help decisions should lead somewhere within three years, against the storm's 1%. The rate of conditions is the setting's, written as a share of storms before any sizing and never tuned toward a count of decisions. A condition touches every page in its neighbourhood, so the reading can see it, but only as a new kind of line if each page says who came for that person and who did not; one line on every page is the festival again (19% of repeat faults). The same old people are exposed at each power cut, so helpers and kinds must vary, or the line repeats by construction (fail-if 4).

**Reach will not move, and the plan's P3 counter is the wrong measure.** Reach (exit 2: 6% today, 15% needed) is the share of weighty events with a weighty descendant naming someone outside the event (`src/tools/consequences.ts:56–57`): 6.7% of about 1,290 a world-decade on the reviewer's re-run. A condition adds about three reaching events; its help acts name helper and helped, and what follows stays between them. So reach goes to about (86 + 3) / (1,290 + 75) ≈ 6.5%. The plan's P3 counter (reach to 15%) would fail a round that works, because it measures spread, not links; this note proposes fail-if 1's link rate, with reach beside it. The plan's personal blow (0.58 per 100 a month) stays as a trigger; a condition is a blow to a set at once. The Hugo turns are not claimed.

## This fails if …

1. **It is upkeep, like the storm.** Sizing check: `environment-scripts/analyse.py`'s `follow`, 40 worlds (9001–9040), on the new help and no-help acts, counting only descendants that change a life (a tie begins or ends, a move, money, a want, a turn) and excluding the condition module's own kinds and a debt called in. Off has no help acts, so the bar is absolute: stop if under 15% lead somewhere within 36 months (the storm 1%, the rescue 40%). If P1 is not kept, `owes` (`world.ts:257`) has no reader, so help sets none and the remembered act carries it alone.
2. **Ties do not decide who is helped.** Sizing check: the share of exposed adults helped within the condition, for those alone and friendless against those with kin or a friend in town; with deciders drawn from `around`, a neighbour can reach the friendless, so the ratio is a result. Stop if it is above 0.7 (help is unequal from the start, Kaniasty and Norris [5]), or if no exposed person is ever left without help.
3. **It only binds.** Sizing check, on the on arm: cooled ties and hostile acts toward the blamed person (a post holder, named by the setting) by those the condition exposed, in the 12 months after a blamed condition against the 12 months before it; stop if the rise does not clear the 20-world interval.
4. **It becomes the festival.** Sizing check, with N1a's counters (so N1a lands before P3's sizing): on the busiest pack's pages, condition lines' copy share must not exceed the page mean (21%), kinds of line per page must rise, and no helper-and-helped pair may appear more than once on a page.
5. **No one can see it, or a director creeps in.** Sizing check: the witness knows at least 30% of help acts (the storm's acts 72–77%, the town 23%); and the guard's lint and import test (`src/tools/guard.ts`, in `npm test`) pass on the condition module, whose chance reads only the setting's rate, the season and the world's physical state.
