# Taking sides

_Research for P2 (`notes/build-plan.md` section 4), 27 October 2026, on `main` at `58d16bb` (round 45 kept, every system on). Throwaway worlds 9001–9040, the 30-year past and ten present years. Script and output: `notes/research/sources/sides-scripts/` (`sides.ts`, `analyse.py`, `analyse-out.txt`). Figures are a mean per world with its 95% interval between worlds, or pooled where said. No engine file was changed, no fresh world was run and nothing was read._

## In short

- **The trigger is there, but it is a third of round 45's volume.** A town has 15.4 ±2.2 rifts a world-decade: a rivalry declared, a split or divorce, a betrayal, a scheme found out, or a falling-out. 7.2 ±1.2 of them have a friend of both sides, and 3.9 have two or more. That gives **14.9 ±3.2 side-takings a world-decade**, one per friend of both, against round 45's 52.7 meetings. Rivalries give 6.3 of them, falling-outs 3.0, splits and divorces 2.8, schemes 1.6 and betrayals 1.3. Forty worlds give 434 side-takings with three years to follow. Today no third person decides anything about a rift, and a friendship's end reaches no one outside the pair (friendship reach 0%, `reports/density-22oct.md`): taking sides is the path by which a rift would reach a third person.
- **The counter as written would pass with nothing built.** Of friends of both, 20.7% already lose the friendship with one side or fall into a rift with them within three years (12.0% of the friendships end, nearly all by losing touch), and 61% share a weighty event with a side. So P2's counter must count only the ties and weighty events that **descend from the side-taking** through cited causes, and it must report the off arm's 20.7% beside it.
- **Liking alone cannot end a tie.** A friend of both likes the less-liked side at a median of 0.63, and knows them well (familiarity near 1). Between people who know each other this well, a friendship ends only when one liking falls below 0.02, or when they lose touch (`social.ts` `checkBond`). A loss of 0.3 in liking ends 3% of these ties, and a loss of 0.4 ends 10%. If the side not taken is to end the friendship, it has to be a decision with a reason, as a betrayal already ends one (`effects.ts`) and a thread ends one for the unforgiving (`threads.ts`). It cannot be a drift in liking.
- **The choice is often close.** The liking gap between the two sides has a median of 0.19, and it is under 0.1 for 32% of friends of both. Fairness, regard and whose account came first therefore decide many of these choices, as the plan intends. Siding by liking alone would leave a third of them to noise.
- **The witness is a party to about one every five years.** Counting the witness as a side or a decider, it is 0.21 ±0.11 side-takings a year, and 22 of 40 witnesses have none in ten years. That matches round 45's 0–4 meetings in six years. At the brief's bar (familiarity 0.1 or more) the number is 1.41 a year, against the town's 1.49. In a town of 83 that bar counts nearly everything, so it measures the town's rate, not what a player sees.
- **Estimate, shrunk: about 19%** of side-takings lead somewhere within three years, counting descent only. The stop is under 15%. Forty worlds give about ±4 points, so the sizing must clear about 20% for the round to run.
- **One life-changing outcome has a path: ended friendships.** Jobs have a thin path, and leaving, moves and partnerships have none (below).

The lesson across them is that sides are either missing, with every opinion pairwise, or decided by a rule: a contract, a threshold or a balance trigger. Balance applied as a rule drives a network to one camp or two (Antal, Krapivsky & Redner 2005). That is fail-if 3 below. **Nemesis** is still the one design that reliably reaches a player, because the player is the object every time (`meeting.md`).

## What the science says

- **Unbalanced triads are rare and get resolved.** In signed networks, two friends of a third who are at odds with each other (the ++− triad) occur far less often than chance, while +++ and +−− occur more often. This holds in Epinions, Slashdot and Wikipedia (Leskovec, Huttenlocher & Kleinberg 2010) and in the Pardus game's friend and enemy layers (Szell, Lambiotte & Thurner 2010). It is the pressure P2 models: the friend of both is in the unstable triad. The literature gives a direction and no effect size we can lift.
- **Siding against a friend costs most, staying neutral costs too, and siding with them gains.** Across three studies, people felt worse toward a friend who sided against them, better toward one who sided with them, and also worse toward one who stayed neutral (Shaw, DeScioli, Barakzai & Kurzban 2017). So "neither" is not free: it should carry a smaller version of the side not taken's cost.
- **Principle excuses disloyalty.** Opposing a friend on stated moral grounds is forgiven more readily than opposing them for no stated reason (_Evolution and Human Behavior_ 47, 2026). This fits Fehr, Gelfand & Nag (2010), where intent goes with forgiveness at r = −.49 and an apology at .42. A side-taking chosen for fairness should leave a weaker resentment than one chosen by liking, and the reason the decider gives is what the side not taken reads.
- **Third parties side with the closer party** (Black's partisanship; DeScioli & Kurzban's alliance hypothesis, 2009). Their side-taking game finds that bystanders coordinate on a side, partly by moral judgement. This supports weighing liking first, with fairness as the counterweight that can override it.
- **Splits cost the shared network.** Most divorcees lose network members soon after a divorce, and half never make up the loss over twelve years; conflict with the ex-partner explains part of it (Terhell, Broese van Groenou & van Tilburg 2004). Divorced people lose friends from the former spouse's side of the network (McDermott, Fowler & Christakis 2013). Shared friends are the ones at stake, and kin are the obvious sides.
- **Embedded ties last longer** (Burt 2000, decay functions). Friends of both sit in exactly the ties that usually protect a friendship, and a rift turns that protection into the strain.

## Outcomes that change lives

Round 25b's what-ifs found that leaving, moves, jobs, partnerships and ended friendships change lives most. The paths from a side-taking to each are these:

- **Ended friendships, with a direct path.** The side not taken ends the friendship when their forgiveness is low, citing the side-taking, as a betrayal and a thread already do. If half to 60% of friends of both choose a side, and a quarter to a third of the people left out are unforgiving enough, then **about 12–20% of side-takings end a friendship**: about 2–3 a world-decade, on top of the friendships ending today. The resentment then seeks the decider through round 45's meeting, which by itself rarely changes a tie (30 of 2,393 meetings).
- **Jobs, with a thin path.** A friend at a workplace puts in a word, but only for someone pursuing work "around" (`work.ts` `wordFor`). A lead's liking of a candidate weighs in hiring (`work.ts`, `liking`). Losing a friend who works somewhere, or a lead's 0.3 of liking, rarely decides a hire. Expected: **under 1%** of side-takings.
- **Leaving, almost no path.** Each friend, up to four, adds 0.04 to how much a person likes their home (`time.ts`), and that liking weighs in leaving. Losing one friend moves it by less than chance does. Expected: **about 0**.
- **Moves and partnerships, no path.** No code reads a friend's side in where someone lives or in whom they partner. A split's friends of both choose between the ex-partners, but that choice changes their friendships, not the partnership. Expected: **0**, unless the design adds a path, which would be its own claim.

So P2 is a round about **who stays friends**, with a resentment that seeks its object. It reaches the turns that readers score (+0.59 z) only through what a lost friend no longer does, such as a word put in or a home liked. It should not be sold as more than that.

**Proposal: report "the witness is a side or a decider, or the witness's friend, partner or kin is among the three, per witness-year" beside P2's counter, never as a target.** The 0.1 bar goes beside it for the record. The witness knows 70% of today's rifts, so knowing of the side-taking itself is fail-if 4.

**A rate too low to be seen.** At 0.2 a year as a party and about 0.7 a year close to one, a player would meet one or two side-takings in a playthrough of a few years. P2 would then change the town and leave exit 5 where it is, as round 45 did. The round is still judged on its counter. Its release says plainly that a player meets it rarely, and Overstory then knows not to build on it as a frequent beat. If the design widens anything, it should widen **who decides**, never how often: someone close to both sides (kin of one and friend of the other, the in-laws at a divorce) faces the same conflict, and the sizing should count those people before choosing the cast. A rare situation reaches a player in one way: the player is put in it. That belongs to the front door (the player as a friend of both is asked), and to Overstory, not to pacing.

## The proposal, for the design job

After a rift, each friend of both (and, if the sizing shows it adds real conflicts, each person close to both) decides **side with one, side with the other, or neither**. The weights are liking for each side, regard, fairness toward the act (who did wrong to whom), and whose account reached them first (`telling.ts`). The reasons name the act. The side not taken loses trust, carries a resentment toward the decider (at less intensity for "neither", and for a choice made on fairness), and ends the friendship if their forgiveness is low, citing the side-taking. The side taken remembers. No timer, count or quota, and nothing reads the triad's balance: balance is what results, never a rule. The decision is queued for the friend in the month after the rift, so it competes with nothing (`meeting.md`'s door). Its weight comes from the liking at stake.

**Estimate.** Half to 60% choose a side. A quarter to a third of those left out end the tie at once: 12% to 20% of side-takings. Where the tie holds, the resentment raises a weighty meeting within three years in about a fifth to a quarter of cases (round 45 raised 20.6% within one year), and the meeting cites the side-taking: 5% to 10%. "Neither" leads somewhere in about 5% of its cases: 1% to 2%. That is 18% to 32%, centre 25%. Shrunk by a quarter, **about 19%**. The stop is under 15%, with the ties alone reported beside the counter.

## This fails if …

1. **Sides are upkeep, or words alone.** Sizing check: the counter, counting descent only, stops under 15%, with the off arm's would-be deciders at 20.7% co-occurrence shown beside it. A power check must show that 40 worlds tell 19% from 15%. If meetings carry over two thirds of the counter, with ties alone under 5%, it changes what is said, not lives.
2. **The choice belongs to the liking, not the person.** Sizing check: in at least 25% of side-takings with a liking gap of 0.1 or more, the side taken is not the one liked more. "Neither" must differ by at least 1.3× between the top and bottom thirds of fairness and of agreeableness. Removing the fairness consideration must flip at least 5% of choices (`flip.ts`).
3. **The town splits into camps** (Antal et al.; round 33's feuds). Sizing check: friendships per adult at year ten within 10% of the off arm's. Reversals at the 90th percentile at most 4 (3.1 today). Round 44's check 4 (wrongs, rows, dislikes, friendships ended per thousand adult-years) within the off arm's interval, or named with its mechanism. These are stops, not targets.
4. **No one can see it.** Sizing check: the witness knows at least 30% of side-takings. The player-visible line above is reported, and if it is under 0.2 a year as a party, the release says so before the reading.
5. **It reads as a script: every rift, the same choosing.** Sizing check: the three answers each take at least 15% of choices. The answer differs by kind of rift (a betrayal against a rivalry). Side-taking lines add no template to the top 25. The copy share does not rise (one-sided bound above +0.6, as round 45).

## What the sizing found: big casts (round 46, 28 Oct 2026)

Round 46 built this and stopped at its sizing, on fail-if 5 (`reports/46-size.md`). The cause was traced in world 9058, whose top 25 held four side-taking templates:

- **Who counts as close to both brings big casts.** Close to both means friends of both, kin including partners, and in-laws at a split. In 9058, 13 rifts drew 9, 9, 5, 5, 4, 3, 3, 3, 3, 2, 1, 1 and 1 deciders.
- **Each decider has a line of their own.** The cut adds two more, one on each page. That world had 15 cuts in ten years.
- **Asking the same person again is not the cause.** Only 4 of 49 side-takings in 9058 were the same decider asked about the same pair again, and 9078 and 9050 had none.

A fix must come from the person's own state, never from a cap or quota on deciders (`notes/build-plan.md`, section 2, the guard). Candidates:

- who is close enough to both to be torn at all (how close, and how lately in touch);
- whether the rift reached the person;
- an answer that leaves no line because nothing changed for the person.

A next round sizes the cast's spread first: the share of rifts with five or more deciders, and the top 25 against cast size.

**The reading.** At 14.9 a world-decade, side-takings touch few pages of a busiest pack. The plan's aimed pack (a rift in both arms) is the reading that can see P2, and the random pack guards the rest.
