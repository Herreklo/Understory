# Feelings and traits

_Research, 22 October 2026, after Øystein's pilot reading. What a feeling should be in the engine, which properties and skills people need, and the rounds that follow. Exit points 5 (worth reading) and 1 (close ties with an inner life). Evidence: four researcher files in `sources/feelings-*.md` (games, emotion science, traits, a code audit), with every source; names in brackets point into them._

## The problem, in our terms

The pilot read Rose Lane and found the acts made sense but the life was samey and what she felt was never said. The audit says why:

- **Feelings are decoration.** 50 `addEmotion` sites write 41 kinds, and no act choice, consideration or situation reads one. `mood()` has one reader, the page panel. Only grief and a new baby change anything, as habit-break multipliers.
- **There is no anger, guilt, fear, shame or pride as a state.** Anger is an act family pulled by patience. A grieving person and an indebted one meet `strain` with the same acts. `p.owes` is declared and never read.
- **The page shows it.** Felt states are 0.7% of 7,450 reasons; 47.6% name only an event or a state ("Steve Moore died"; "age 63"); "it hit hard" says nothing about what was felt or for whom. The 25 commonest lines are 22% of all lines, and friendships starting or ending 12%.
- **What does work as feeling today is bonds and stress:** liking, respect and trust toward a named person (54 sites, 8% of reasons) and stress with named sources. They read best because they have an object.

So the engine has the slot and the wording sites, and nothing behind them. A person's 25 facets reach the page through one good-or-bad number and a handful of answers.

## What games did, and where it went wrong

| System | What a feeling is | Where it went wrong |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The Sims 4 | 16 emotions from moodlets with timers of hours | drives the player's menu, not the Sim; no Sim "makes a bold decision because of their emotions" (PC Gamer); easy to farm; the best-known mods add inertia |
| RimWorld | one mood bar fed by 350+ timed thoughts; breaks below thresholds | the break is picked by severity, not cause, so the letter blames "ate without table" in a firefight |
| Crusader Kings III | stress from acting against one's traits; breaks with trait-marked options; at most one per five years | one number for every cost; coping traits picked as a build |
| Dwarf Fortress | 130 emotions, each with its circumstance; 8 + 8 memory slots relived through the year; core memories shift facets | only 10 of 130 have a behaviour; the rest feed one stress number; in well-kept forts under 1% are unhappy |
| Football Manager | hidden 1–20 personality, morale in words, groups | numbers never shown, labels instead (works); press and team talks repetitive and read as random |
| Versu | one feeling at a time, with target and cause; click a portrait for who and why | agents scoring few options behave alike; 1–2 months to write 20 minutes; cancelled |
| Prom Week | 34 expiring statuses over 5,000 rules, shown as meters | causes too diffuse to narrate; meters invited manipulation |
| PsychSim, EMA, GAMYGDALA, FAtiMA | appraisals with blame and control; GAMYGDALA's 16 OCC kinds | GAMYGDALA changes no act by design; PsychSim's explanations raised trust from 5.4 to 6.2 of 7; FAtiMA retreated to authored structure |
| Talk of the Town; Nemesis | a grieving flag; orcs with scars, grudges and callbacks | in Talk of the Town, beliefs carried the story, not feeling; Nemesis is what players retell |

Six rules follow, each from more than one system:

1. **A kind earns its place only through acts it alone pulls toward:** at most three families per kind, one of them its own. Otherwise it is wording (Dwarf Fortress, GAMYGDALA, the Sims).
2. **The response is chosen by cause, target and whether the person can do anything, never by size;** size decides only whether to respond (RimWorld against CK3 and EMA).
3. **Two layers:** feelings with a target act; mood has none and only bends. One summed bar made RimWorld and Dwarf Fortress samey.
4. **Cap and group:** about eight live feelings a person, one per kind and target, strongest kept, so an irritation cannot dilute a death. Versu's one-at-a-time override assumes scene time; on a month it would erase grief.
5. **Habituation and lasting causes,** or the town converges on fine: good things fade with repetition; a feeling tied to a lasting fact holds until the fact changes or the person copes.
6. **Words with a target and a cause, never meters;** and a strong feeling left unresolved changes the person, with its cause and year (Dwarf Fortress's core memories, CK3's coping traits, Nemesis scars).

## The science, used

- **Which feeling** comes from how an event is seen: does it matter to me, who caused it, is it settled or pending, can it be repaired, does it break a standard, whose fortune is it (OCC; Scherer; Lazarus). The links are probabilistic, so they are weights that personality moves, not rules.
- **What it makes people do.** Four directions (Roseman): towards (gratitude, pride), away (grief, worry, regret), against (anger), and rejecting (shame, contempt). Whether the harm can be repaired matters more than the guilt/shame label: across 90 samples, shame approaches when the failure is reparable (g = .47) and avoids when not (g = −.34) [Leach & Cidam 2015]. Gratitude predicts repaying the giver (r = .37 across 91 studies). Anger is short-term attack, long-term repair: targets reported the relationship stronger in 48% of episodes, weaker in 35% [Averill].
- **How long.** No episode lasts a month (median 2–5 hours; sadness about 120). What lasts months is a standing appraisal that keeps firing: the cause is still there, the person is met again, it is dwelt on [Verduyn & Lavrijsen 2015; Frijda]. **So a monthly feeling persists by renewal, not by a timer.** Month-scale data exist for three: grief (Bonanno: 46% resilient, 11% common grief, 16% chronic, 4% delayed), grudges (forgiveness fast then a long tail, within about three months; faster where the bond is worth keeping) and regret (actions fade, inactions grow).
- **How it is handled.** Distraction and reappraisal help (d = .27, .36); dwelling hurts (d = −.26); suppression hides the face, not the feeling [Webb et al. 2012].
- **Feelings are news.** 80–95% of emotional episodes are told to others, 58% the same day, mostly to close people; two thirds of listeners retell it [Rimé]. In our terms a feeling is something the town comes to know.
- **Mood is derived and has no object; a feeling has one** (ALMA, EMA, GAMYGDALA). Our mood is fine; our feelings lack object and kind.
- **Weak spots.** The kinds are design categories, not natural kinds. Action tendencies predict acts weakly; fear and anger on risk replicated only in part (2024). Month durations other than the three above are extrapolated. Shares come from Western samples: starting odds, never targets.

## Traits: how hard they push, and who can see them

- **Size.** A 1-SD trait difference should move a single decision by r ≈ .1–.3 and a life outcome by .1–.2 [Gignac & Szodorai; Soto 2019; Roberts et al. 2007]. Above .5 people become predictable.
- **Feelings are mostly situation.** For forgiveness, the offender's intent (r −.49), an apology (.42) and empathy (.51) outweigh agreeableness (.22) [Fehr et al. 2010]. Personality sets four knobs per kind (proneness, intensity, duration, handling); the event does the rest.
- **The knobs, from facets we have:** anger from low patience, low gentleness, low flexibility (HEXACO puts anger in low agreeableness, not emotionality); grief from sentimentality, chronic with dependence; guilt with sincerity, fairness and altruism; shame from low social self-esteem, turning aggressive only through blaming others; envy from low self-esteem in a domain one cares about, malicious with low honesty-humility; worry from anxiety, lowered by organisation.
- **Who can see what** (self–other agreement, Connelly & Ones 2010): extraversion shows to strangers (.22), emotional stability hardly at all (.08) until family (.43). How well the player knows someone should cap what they can read, by this table.

## Properties and skills

**The test.** A property earns its place only if it (1) changes a decision or how someone is read, (2) shares under half its variance with what exists, (3) predicts something that matters at r ≥ .10 beyond it, (4) can be read by a player, or its consequences, within a year, (5) yields a story the current set cannot, and (6) is one number made from what the person has.

**Cut, in a neutral change:** `selfBias`, `owes` and `lastProvoked` have no reader; `signatures` and `tension` drive nothing after birth.

**Give a job, or cut:** creativity (12 references) and unconventionality (23). Perfectionism, social self-esteem and greed-avoidance get work from the feeling knobs.

**Change, as experiments:** values follow traits at r ≈ .55–.6, against modest links in the literature: cap at .4. Skills never decay: lose about half the gain a year unused (physical skills slower), and gain in proportion to practice × (ceiling − rating), which gives plateaus [Tatel & Ackerman 2025; Arthur et al. 1998].

**Add, later:** attachment avoidance, one number (the Big Five explain 12% of it, and it predicts relationships better than they do).

**General skills.** Two pass the test and belong in the engine, because every setting has people in it:

- **Reading people**, its ceiling from social insight: how fast and how truly beliefs about another's feeling, intent or honesty update; who notices grief or a lie before it is said; who gets fooled. Friends read each other about half again as well as strangers, and person-perception training works [Stinson & Ickes; Blanch-Hartigan 2012]. It multiplies the acquaintance cap, it does not replace it. It is also the player's own task, which makes it the natural test of skill-based play.
- **Keeping one's head**, starting from patience and low anxiety and raised by hard feelings lived through with support: it is the handling knob (lashing out, hiding, how long worry lasts).

Not engine skills: self-control (diligence and prudence renamed; training g = .13–.24 after bias correction), a single emotional-intelligence score (unreliable), persuasion and comforting (setting skills built on presence, social insight and reading people). Trade skills stay in the setting, as they are.

## The proposal

**A feeling** holds a kind, its cause (an event), its object (a person, a prospect or the self), an intensity, and whether the cause still stands. Each month it fades at its kind's rate unless renewed, and repeated good things fade faster. A person holds at most eight, one per kind and object. While it lives, it adds weight to at most three act families in any situation that involves its object, one of them its own, and the act within the family follows its cause and whether the person can do anything about it. When it decides, it is the reason, worded with its object: "still sore at Ralph over the expense claims", not "it hit hard". One left unresolved past its kind's horizon becomes a lasting change, with its cause and year. Mood stays as it is, with no object.

**The first four kinds** are the ones with a person as object, because a feeling about someone is a story and a callback, and they pull in four different directions:

| Kind | Trigger, from events we already have | Pull (families that exist) | Fades | Knobs from |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Grief | a death, a break-up, a lost job or home | withdrawal, seeking a few, reluctance; later restoration | trajectory drawn at onset (Bonanno shares) | sentimentality, dependence |
| Resentment | a named person wronged me or mine, unrepaired | withdrawal from them, severity, anger; refusal | fast, long tail; renewed on meeting; an apology ends it | patience, forgiveness, gentleness |
| Guilt | my act harmed someone I value | humility (apologise), honesty, generosity | ends when amends are accepted; else 1–3 months | sincerity, fairness, altruism |
| Gratitude | another's costly help, not owed | generosity and warmth toward the giver, duty | until repaid; leaves a bond gain | altruism, sentimentality |

The 50 existing `addEmotion` sites already say which event gives which feeling; they gain a kind, an object and readers. Resentment and guilt give the never-read `owes` its job. Five families that appear in two or fewer situation types (disclosure, discretion, openness, reluctance, severity) get pulled by feelings instead.

**Second, if the first is kept:** worry (a pending bad outcome: defer, save, avoid risk), shame (hide or restore standing, by whether it can be repaired), pride, envy and regret. Left out at a monthly scale: anything too brief to cross a tick (fright, surprise, embarrassment), endings that are not states (relief, disappointment), and what bonds, needs or mood already hold (love, contempt, loneliness, joy, hope).

**Estimate.** In the round 40 pack, 48% of lines (1,639 of 3,429) cite an earlier event as a reason; those are where a named feeling would replace a bare event. Shrunk for lines whose event carries no feeling, I expect 15–30% of decision lines to change act or reason, and the commonest-25 share to fall by a few points, not more: friendships starting and ending are not feelings.

## The rounds, in order

1. **Neutral, now:** cut the readerless fields; name the secret in "carrying something nobody knew"; say who the dead person was to the subject; give the habit line its job-loss event. These change words or nothing.
2. **A small fix round:** illness that recurs gets worse and counts (no endless strokes); the `work` act only for people with work.
3. **Feelings, part one:** the four kinds above, behind `OFF=feelings`. Moves exit points 5 and 1.
4. **Feelings, part two,** if part one is kept: worry, shame, pride, envy, regret, and keeping one's head as the handling knob.
5. **Reading people,** with the acquaintance cap, once the accounts arc is back (it needs beliefs about others' feelings).
6. **Skills that decay and plateau; values loosened;** each its own round, since each changes lives.

Which of these enters "Next", and where, is Øystein's call.

## This fails if …

1. **Everyone is always sore at someone.** Sizing check: the share of adult-months carrying each kind; stop if any kind is active in over a quarter of adults, or grief in more than its Bonanno shares allow.
2. **Feelings add reasons but change no acts,** as round 32's friendship record decided under 1%. Sizing check: the share of decisions where removing the feeling flips the family; stop below 5%.
3. **It adds a new repeat.** "Still sore at Ralph" eight times in a life is the old problem in new words. Sizing check: the top-25 line share and the median repeats per version must not rise.
4. **Resentment escalates into feuds the town does not otherwise have.** Sizing check: rows, ended friendships and retaliation per thousand adult-years stay within the switch-off interval, or the round says plainly that it changes them.
5. **Readers still cannot say what someone felt.** Sizing check: on the sizing pages, every line a feeling decided names the kind and the object (mechanical), and a single cheap sitting can name what three people felt about whom.
