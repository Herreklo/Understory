# Round 33: independent re-derivation of the headline numbers

Method: own Python (scipy t quantiles) over the raw files; summary.md used only to know which claims to check. Scripts in
`$S/audit33/` (board.py, cons.py, story.py, friends.py, reckon.py, rerun_pw.py with rerun_a.sh and rerun_b.sh, tests.py, blind.py, pack.py; `out-*.txt` are their printed results; rerun/ holds the reckon.ts outputs); S = the scratchpad. Interval method for all
world-level numbers: mean over worlds, 95% half-width = t(0.975, n-1) x sd / sqrt(n); the 90% interval uses t(0.95, n-1).

Notes, not disagreements

- Claim 1: nothing is distinguishable from zero at 95%. Negative worlds: 11 of 20 on all decisions (range -7.2 to +4.8), 8 of 20 not a couple's (-7.3 to +5.5), 12 of 20 key (-10.6 to +4.4); paired t p = 0.967 / 0.730 / 0.188.
  The all-decisions and not-a-couple 90% intervals lie inside +-2 (the not-a-couple upper end is 1.86); the key slice 90% interval (-2.75 to 0.32) reaches past -2, consistent with "not shown either way".
  The on arm has 3.1% more decisions overall (34465 vs 33441) and 9.3% more in the key slice (17487 vs 15994). The reckoning's own decisions are 311 `reckon` plus 38 `reckon-answer` in the key slice (350 and 188 overall), so they are 2% of it; leaving the triggers that exist only in the on arm out of the key slice gives -1.30 +-1.89 (90%: -2.86 to 0.26; 17134 / 15994), no change in reading.
  Calibrated minus notebook on the key slice, on minus off paired: -0.63 +-1.51; the gap is above zero in both arms (4.86 +-0.94 and 5.49 +-1.30), which is the gate's second condition.
- Claim 2: the rounded "0.05 vs 0.06" is 0.0531 vs 0.0551, a gap of 0.002 that rounding turns into a step (paired -0.0020 +-0.0040). "0.24 vs 0.23" is +0.0094 +-0.0094, right on the edge of zero (p 0.052); the on arm also has 4.6% more sources (17905 vs 17120), so both numbers move with how many weighty events there are to follow. Nothing here shows reach differing.
- Claim 3: the one story measure whose paired interval excludes zero is dead lives: fewer with the switch on (-0.0145 +-0.0139, p 0.041; 40 vs 55 of 1038 adults each). Both arms have exactly 1038 adults in total, but per world the counts differ (for example 3220: 54 on, 44 off), so the equal total is chance. The median "17.9 vs 17.1" holds for the tool's upper median per world (17.85 vs 17.10; 17.85 sits on the rounding line and prints 17.9); with true medians it is 17.6 vs 17.0, and the paired difference (+0.75 +-1.12) is within noise. Arcs 0.47 vs 0.43 are +0.040 +-0.116 paired.
- Claim 4: the median "4 vs 3" is on a knife edge. The on arm's median is the 670th of 1340 adults; 667 have three close friends or fewer (49.8%), so three more adults with 3 or fewer would make it 3 vs 3. The off arm has 52.1% at 3 or fewer. The means (4.45 vs 4.12) are the steadier reading: paired +0.32 +-0.33 (p 0.059). Against the pre-registered "within +-1 of main's" the median is exactly at the limit (+1) and the mean is +0.3.
  Ended per adult a year is not different (paired +0.010 +-0.030, p 0.50; round 32 had +0.041 +-0.031). The tool's "ended" is a net count (made + start - end), not a count of cooling events, and its per-adult figure is 2 x pairs / adult-years. Young adults with no friend in town 26.5% of 136 vs 21.2% of 146 (Fisher p 0.33; bootstrap over worlds of the pooled difference +5.2 points, 95% [-3.4, +13.3]): a point estimate, not a shown rise.
- Claim 5: "followed" has no comparison arm: the off arm has no reckonings and keeps no record (eligible 0 and carry-citing endings 0 in extra/reckon-off.txt; src/carry.ts `carryOn = ON.has('carry') || reckonOn()`), so 47.6% cannot be set against what happens to one-sided friendships left alone. 147 of the 166 followed reckonings are `balanced` (the record stops being one-sided, which includes the favour given); 159 of 334 counts only reckonings with a full year left, 166 of 370 counts all.
  Endings: cooled endings are +9.5% (657 vs 600) and endings over every path +7.2% (1254 vs 1170), with 1.1% fewer adult-years in the on arm, so +8.3% per adult-year. The reference has totals only; from my 40 single-seed runs, paired by world, endings per adult-year are +0.0050 +-0.0089 (p 0.25; 13 of 20 worlds higher, on mean 0.0676 vs off 0.0626), cooled per adult-year +0.0035 +-0.0043 (p 0.10), dated share of cooled +0.003 +-0.035. So the +8% is a point estimate, not a shown rise. The dated share 15.4% vs 15.2% is 101 of 657 against 91 of 600: at the off share the on arm would have had about 100, so it differs by about 1 ending.
  The 15 endings that "cite the record" are pattern matches ("was always the one who", "had always been the one who", "had stopped going round") in a cause label or the ending text; the totals do not say how many are round 32's record and how many follow a reckoning.
- Claim 7: eight of the twelve on-arm lives contain no reckoning line at all. The 16 reckoning lines in the pack (6 "told ... straight that", 10 "asked ... for something back, for once", 2 "took it to heart" answers; "stopped going round" 0) are in four pairs (P03 2, P04 2, P06 2, P07 10), and none appears in an off version (so the key and the pack agree on every one of them). In those four pairs the mean on-off score difference is +0.33; in the other eight it is -0.29. So most of the reading compares two lives that diverged for other reasons, and the pairs that carry a reckoning are too few (four) to say anything about it. Four of the sixteen reckoning lines carry "Nobody was surprised any more: it had been happening since ...", as BOARD says; that phrase also occurs in off versions (14 lines) and is not a mark of the arm.
  Preference: the on arm got 10 of 36 votes; the off arm was preferred by majority in 9 of 12 pairs (exact two-sided p 0.146 for 3 of 12, one-sided 0.073; the three readers see the same pairs, so the 36 choices are not independent). Scores alone: on higher in 10 reader-pairs, off higher in 15, tied in 11 (sign test p 0.42). The `prefer` field never contradicts the scores (0 of 36) and decides the 11 score ties, all 11 for the off arm (in six pairs: P04, P05, P07, P08, P09, P10; 4 of the 11 chose B), so the preference count is lower than the score difference alone would suggest. The order was dealt six and six; B scored 0.14 higher than A on average and was chosen in 20 of 36 (56%). Split by order, on-minus-off is -0.22 where on was A (6 pairs, majority on in 1) and +0.06 where on was B (6 pairs, majority on in 2).
  Quoted by readers: 8 quotes contain the reckoning's or round 32's wording (reckoning 4: 2 as a good reason, 2 as an unfit reason; round 32's record 4: 1 unfit reason, 1 false line, 2 wording); all 8 are on-arm text. A reader could in principle have told the arm from this wording, but the readers' reasons do not show that they did. The key and the A/B texts were taken as given; the assignment was not regenerated.
- Claim 8: single runs of 20 worlds each, no interval printed, and the test files hold no per-world values, so none of the differences can be tested here. Twists -12.5% crosses the "not down by more than a tenth" line by 2.5 points. Population 83 vs 85, adults 67 vs 68, splits 4.2 vs 4.7, fires 1.3 vs 0.8, quits 1.3 vs 1.9, honest-why base 35322 vs 34665 decisions.
- The re-derivation checks reproducibility and aggregation of the tools' counts; it does not re-derive what reckon.ts counts as one-sided, followed, a record-citing ending or a dated reason, and it did not rebuild the calibrated predictions (`cal`) from the engine; those are the recorded values in the eval files.
