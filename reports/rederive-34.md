Notes, not disagreements

- Claim 1: nothing is distinguishable from zero. Negative worlds: 11 of 20 on all decisions (range -6.4 to +4.3), 12 of 20 not a couple's (-7.9 to +3.6), 10 of 20 key (-5.9 to +7.6); paired t p = 0.58 / 0.42 / 0.60.
  "Equivalent within +-2" rests on the 90% intervals, which lie inside +-2 in all three rows. The 95% interval of the key slice (-2.19 to +1.29) reaches past -2; the all-decisions (-1.70 to 0.98) and not-a-couple (-1.70 to 0.74) 95% intervals do not.
  The on arm has 3.9% more decisions overall (34487 vs 33206) and 5.6% more in the key slice (17665 vs 16723). The difference is the aftermath's own decisions: 1483 `aftermath` decisions exist only in the on arm (4.3% of its decisions, 932 of them in the key slice = 5.3%),
  and the calibrated witness is right on only 49.8% of them (base rate 48.2%, notebook 40.7%, page reader 49.4%, the mind-reader 75.3%) against 62.3% on the on arm's other decisions. Leaving the triggers that exist only in the on arm out (aftermath, plus 4 "whether @ should be owner one day" and 1 "whether to allow the café") gives
  all decisions +0.17 +-1.32 (90%: -0.92 to 1.26; 32999 / 33206), not a couple's +0.59 +-1.25 (90%: -0.44 to 1.62), key +0.21 +-1.75 (90%: -1.24 to 1.65; 16733 / 16723). So the slightly negative point estimates come from the new decisions being harder to predict, not from a change in the older ones.
  Calibrated minus notebook on the key slice, on minus off paired: -0.82 +-1.78 (90%: -2.29 to +0.65); the gap is above zero in both arms (4.39 +-1.09 and 5.21 +-1.05), which is the gate's second condition.

- Claim 5: "followed" is a share of blows with a change in the life within three years, measured alike in both arms (same blowsOf), but the on-arm change includes the aftermath's own acts: a takeup forms a routine, a keepaway breaks one, a startover leaves the job, and the measure counts exactly those things (job, partner, routines, music, group, being in town). So the +17 points show that the acts register in lives, not that lives change for other reasons.
  Every one of the ten blow types is higher on than off (caught 80.0% vs 55.6%, cooled 53.9% vs 44.4%, death 74.6% vs 47.6%, divorce 76.2% vs 52.4%, dressdown 87.5% vs 69.6%, dismiss 100% vs 83.3%, passed 91.7% vs 66.7%, rake 72.3% vs 50.1%, split 70.3% vs 42.9%, want:failed 83.9% vs 72.4%); the small types (dismiss 10 and 12, passed 12 and 3, divorce 21 and 21) are single digits or low tens.
  By what changed (on vs off): routines 648 vs 445, job 252 vs 163, partner 150 vs 92, being in town 73 vs 44, group 47 vs 34, music 13 vs 15. The "being in town" count (status differs from 'in' three years later: away, dead, bg or gone; src/tools/diag/aftermath.ts `mark`) is 66% higher in the on arm; the raw files do not say which of these it is, and the total count of blows is similar (1206 vs 1223). Worth looking at before reading startover as free.
  The 1458 decisions are not the 1206 followed blows: decisions cover blows in all ten years while the follow-up keeps only blows in the first seven, a person gets at most one aftermath a year (`tried['aftermath']` within 12 months is skipped), and a blow with no possible answer gets none (same age floor of 16 in both). The off arm's 50.0% is the baseline: half of all blows are followed by some change in a life with no aftermath at all.
- Claim 7: the mechanism does not raise jobless share; the point estimate is lower. Students are zero at the end of the window in both arms, so "not students" does nothing here. - Claim 8: the on arm is B in 12 of 24 and the order was dealt evenly. Scores alone: on higher in 22 reader-pairs, off higher in 32, tied in 18 (sign test on the 54 untied, p 0.22); pair means: on higher in 10 pairs, off higher in 12, tied 2. Mean score on 3.07, off 3.19 (all 72 readings). B scored 0.10 higher than A on average (3.18 vs 3.08) and was chosen in 57%.
  Split by order, on-minus-off is -0.22 where on was A (12 pairs, majority on in 5) and -0.03 where on was B (12 pairs, majority on in 6). The `prefer` field never contradicts the scores (0 of 72). It decides the 18 score ties: 11 for the on arm, 7 for the off arm (in round 33 all 11 ties went to off), so the 11 of 24 is helped by the ties; counting scores only, on is ahead in 10 of 24 pair means. Exact two-sided p for 11 of 24 at null 1/2 is 0.84.
  The 24 pair means are the unit of the stated interval (+-0.40, t over 23 df). Treating the 72 reader-pairs as independent gives +-0.24 (70 df), which is too narrow because the three readers see the same pairs; clustering by pair gives +-0.40 (23 df), as the pair-mean fit does. The one-sided 90% lower bound -0.38 says the true effect is not below -0.38 points on the 5-point scale at that confidence; zero stays inside every version.
  Faults quoted: on 215 (false line 69, unfit reason 61, repeat 39, out of nowhere 27, wording 19), off 216 (false line 61, repeat 49, unfit reason 47, wording 30, out of nowhere 29). The on arm has 14 more unfit-reason faults and 10 fewer repeats.
  Key check against the pack text: the aftermath's own acts carry reasons that no other mechanism writes ("it hit hard", "it had shaken them", "it happened at work"; src/aftermath.ts); 12 lines carry them in the pack, all in the version the key calls on and none in the version it calls off (24 pairs). So most of the reading compares two lives that diverged for other reasons.
  In the 10 pairs with a visible aftermath line the mean on-off score is -0.07; in the other 14 it is -0.17. Readers quoted the aftermath wording 13 times, all in on-arm text: 6 as a good reason (P07, P09 x2, P13, P14, P19), 6 as an unfit reason (P02 x3, P07 x2, P16) and 1 as wording (P05).
