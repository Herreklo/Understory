Person Model Lab · scale track · base round 19c

City Scale

From a 75-person town to a city of 100,000. In the monthly world, people meet through settings instead of the whole town. Every neighbourhood has its own workplaces, and work is bounded by the jobs there are. The rest of the city lives once a year: by the engine's own rules for what is one person's alone, and by odds learned from the engine for what depends on others. A neighbourhood comes into focus when the player's story reaches it, and nothing outside it changes its past.

S0 · Measure

Where the town stops scaling

The engine was timed system by system at four sizes, from 68 to about 2,628 people. Each size had the same five-year past and twelve timed months, and runs went one at a time. Cost grows with population to the power 1.95. At the largest size a month takes 17.4 s, building the world takes 11 minutes, and memory holds 2.4 million beliefs in 1,414 MB.

The causes are loops over the whole town, done for everyone. Company is sought from every adult, each scored by regard, and this alone is 30% of all time at 1,000 people. The others:

  • school pairs over every child, 70,000 contacts a month at 2,600 people;
  • a family scan over every pair;
  • public news heard by everyone over 14;
  • workplace lookups that scan the town once per place;
  • history re-read every month.

Each dot is one world. Lines join the means at each size. The dashed line is linear growth from the smallest branch world. Branch figures are from the S2b runs.

S2 · S7 · The social layer at scale

Many towns, each with its own work

The branch splits a city into neighbourhoods of about 80 people, roughly one town each.

  • A household lives together, and moves when people move in together.
  • School, routines, hobbies, romance and the company people seek happen inside a neighbourhood.
  • Every neighbourhood has its own workplaces: one of each kind the setting has, each with the kind's capacity, its staff and its leader. A workplace takes on people from its own neighbourhood first, then from across the city, and a venture opens where its founder lives.
  • News reaches the neighbourhoods of the people involved.
  • No code creates a bond with a stranger just by looking at them.

Per person, the branch does the same work at every size: about 8 contacts a month, 60 bonds and 225 beliefs, from 270 people to 2,640. What still grows is the cost of each lookup in bigger maps. That is why the exponent stops at 1.20 rather than 1.0.

S1 · Design

Detail follows what the player could know

The player only ever sees the felt side of the world. Someone the player cannot reach needs to be true, not detailed. Everyone in the city is a full person, with traits, values, family and a seed. Only how often they are simulated differs.

in focus · monthly

Every system runs

Whole neighbourhoods: the player's own and every one the player's story reaches. Typically 1,000 to 3,000 people.

in view · as counterparts · designed

People in the room

A colleague at a big workplace, or the bar staff at a regular venue. Full people form bonds with them. They make no decisions of their own until their neighbourhood comes into focus.

background · yearly

A year lived at once

They grow older, fall ill, work, lead, partner, have children, leave, come home or die. Every big event is written into the record, and nothing outside their neighbourhood changes it.

S3 · S7 · S8 · The background

A background that lives by the engine's rules

A background year has two kinds of rule.

  • What is one person's alone runs by the engine's own formulas, month by month through the year. That covers growing older (personality maturing, aptitudes and skill ceilings moving with age), health, illness, retirement, death, the strain that is the person's own, and the steadying that comes with becoming a parent. S7 found why this matters. Odds fitted to these drift as soon as the population they are applied to drifts. The background had never matured, because the engine's yearly step ran only over the monthly world, and its old people died at half the engine's rate.
  • What depends on other people is drawn from odds learned from the engine: finding a partner, splitting up, a child, leaving or coming home, drinking, losing a job. Thirty full towns were logged year by year, and the odds were checked on towns the fit never saw. One script refits them after any engine change.
  • Work comes from the institutions. Each year a background workplace with room takes on the best-suited job seeker of its neighbourhood. A slot nobody local can fill draws a newcomer, by the town's own rule: that is the only way people arrive, as in the town. A workplace without a leader makes one, with the town's succession events: the promotion, the first staff meeting, and those passed over.

S3 · S8 · Promotion

A neighbourhood comes into focus

When the player's story reaches someone, their whole neighbourhood comes into focus, so everyone in it arrives among the people they live with. A background life has a spine and a texture.

  • The spine is every big event with its date. It is fixed once lived and stands as it is.
  • The texture is the small things that make someone known: friends, dislikes, routines. It is made at promotion. The years they lived there are replayed by the engine's own contact rule over their settings, on a clock set back to when those months happened, so every rule that counts time sees months.

The neighbourhood's workplaces come into focus with their staff and leaders. Marks such as fears, sore spots, pride and rules to live by follow from the spine, through the same rules as everyone else's.

S11 · The twin test

Does a background life leave the same person?

Four cities from one founding: the same people, the same traits and families at year 0. One neighbourhood lives its thirty-year past in full (A), in full on other dice (A2, a different full life), or in the background and is then promoted (B). The founding itself is the fourth (Z, the same people thirty years younger). Every adult founder who is alive in all of them is asked the engine's sixteen kinds of situation. The answers are built and scored by the engine's own decision rules, from the person's true state.

The question is how far each copy's answers moved from who the person was at the founding. If the background keeps a life, B moves as far as A and A2.

100 twins over four fresh cities. Distances are total variation on the answer probabilities, and disagreement on the acts.

What it shows

A person who spent thirty years in the background carries about seven eighths of what thirty years of full life change in how they choose. In every city, B moved less than both full lives. The missing eighth fits what monthly life forms and the background does not: learned if-then habits (about a quarter fewer) and the self-story people tell about who they are (a fifth shorter).

Two limits. In these situations a lived life moves answers only about 7% of the distance between two people, because inborn traits set most of them. And the battery cannot see strain, money, bonds or marks. Testing those needs the engine's real situations, which belongs after the merge with the lab's engine.

The first version of this test (S11) used a bar that could not fail. The independent check caught it, and the directional measure here (S11b) replaced it.

S8 · Independence

Nothing outside a neighbourhood changes its past

A background neighbourhood draws everything from itself:

  • its dice, keyed to each person or to the neighbourhood and year;
  • the ids and first names of people born or arriving there;
  • partners from its own residents;
  • workers from its own job seekers;
  • newcomers for its own vacancies.

The bonds its people hold are put away with them until they come back into focus, because no contact happens in the background.

The test builds two cities from one seed. In the second, the monthly world lives a different thirty-year past and a different present, and one background neighbourhood is promoted. Every other background neighbourhood must come out identical: every field of every person, every event about them, every bond and belief they hold, and their workplaces.

What the background costs a month

The pre-registered bar, within 50% of 0.41 ms per person, fails: 0.66 ms. The independent check found that S4 and S7 had divided by the wrong number of people, and they fail too (0.72 and 0.74 ms). The bar also compared unlike towns: the 0.41 reference had a five-year past, the city a thirty-year one.

The same 240-person town timed alone and inside 100,000, alternating, one run at a time: alone 0.87, 0.62, 0.59 ms per person; in the city 0.88, 0.80, 0.71. By the medians the background adds about 30% to an ordinary month, likely garbage collection on a larger heap. That is within the noise between runs.

Absolute timings depend on the machine. After a restart onto a slower host, the same code built this city in about 200 s instead of 138 s, so timings are compared only within one sitting.

The cost

What the background does not keep, and what is unresolved

  • Small acts and their evidence. A promoted person arrives with friends and dislikes, but nobody has seen them do anything. The player starts from nothing about them, as with any stranger.
  • Marks from small public moments. Being shown up, let down or found out leaves a mark only in focus. Promoted over-40s carry about half to two thirds of the marks.
  • Habits of mind formed by monthly life. Learned if-then habits and self-story come from living in focus. Promoted people have about a quarter fewer if-thens and a fifth less self-story, and the twin test shows about an eighth of a life's change in their choices missing.
  • Strain from other people. Grief, loneliness and trouble at home are not lived in the background.
  • Texture depends on when. The same neighbourhood promoted in a different year has the same spine, but its friendships are drawn afresh. Nobody had seen them.
  • Backstory dates. Friendships formed during catch-up enter the record dated to the promotion and marked as backstory, because the record must stay in time order.
  • Unresolved: the month's cost at 100,000. It is about 25% over the same town alone, near the noise between runs.
  • Unresolved: drinking. The background drinks at about 1.5 times the monthly rate. That is within the 5-point bar, but a real difference.
  • Not built: demotion. A neighbourhood that leaves the story stays in focus.
  • The engine has moved on. The lab is past round 23 and has reformatted its code. This branch is round 19c and will be carried onto the lab's next archive.
For the engine

The scaling contract

Rules for new engine code, so it scales and a town stays exactly as it is. The full text is in the project, at claude/scale/contract.md.

  1. People meet through settings, never through the town. Household, workplace, school class, neighbourhood, venue, group, or the person's own ties.
  2. No loop over people inside a loop over people. A step for one person or one event takes others from that person's settings.
  3. Looking at a bond must not create one.
  4. News travels by setting and reach. For someone notable, reach should grow with fame. Fame then becomes how far news about you travels.
  5. Read recent history from the end.
  6. Membership changes are tracked, so indexes stay right without rebuilding.
  7. Dice belong to people. A background life never depends on who else is simulated.
  8. Ids must fit a city. Bond keys are widened to room for 67 million people.
  9. A rule about one person is written once. Put it in a function that takes the person, so the background can live it too, as the yearly maturing step now is.
  10. Workplaces are asked for by kind. Code that wants "the bar" asks for the bar in a person's neighbourhood, never for a fixed id.
  11. The town stays the town. Every scaling change passes the identity test.
Method

How it was measured, and what was not

  • Machine and runs. Two cores and 7 GB, Node 22 with tsx. Timed runs ran one at a time.
  • Workplaces. Until S4, harnesses copied the setting's eight places in proportion to the town. Since S7, every neighbourhood has its own set and nothing is copied.
  • What a system's time means. It runs from the moment the monthly tick switches to that system's random stream until the next switch. The engine is not edited to measure it.
  • The branch. Round 19c plus the changes listed in docs/branch-changes.md. The lab's own files were not touched. All work lives in the scale folder and in claude/scale/.
  • Seeds. Every step's test runs on fresh seeds, after smoke runs on throwaway ones. Each step's tree is frozen before its runs.

Not measured:

  • speed in a browser, where the game may run;
  • monthly worlds larger than about 2,700 people;
  • memory over many decades;
  • the in-view tier and fame-driven reach, which are designed but not built;
  • a human cold read of promoted lives against always-full ones.
