S0 · Measure

Where the town stops scaling

The engine was timed system by system at four sizes, from 75 to about 2,600 people. Each world had the same five-year past, then twelve timed months. Its cost grows with population to the power {{EXP_E}}. The social tick alone grows at {{SEXP_E}}. At the largest size a month takes {{BIG_E}} seconds, building the world takes {{BUILD_E}} minutes, and memory holds {{BEL_E}} million beliefs in {{HEAP_E}} MB.

The causes are loops over the whole town, done for everyone:

  • company sought from every adult, each scored by regard;
  • school pairs over every child;
  • a family scan over every pair;
  • public news heard by everyone over 14;
  • workplace lookups that scan the town once per place;
  • gossip and the collection re-reading the whole history every month.

S2 · The social layer at scale

Many Skarviks, joined by work

The branch splits a city into neighbourhoods of about 80 people, roughly one Skarvik each. A household lives together and moves when people move in together. School, routines, hobbies and the company people seek happen inside a neighbourhood. Workplaces and groups join neighbourhoods together. News reaches the neighbourhoods of the people involved. Reactions to someone come from the people around them. Nothing creates a bond with a stranger just by looking at them.

In the branch, cost grows at {{EXP_B}}, and the social tick at {{SEXP_B}}. Pre-registered: total at most 1.15 {{EXP_OK}}, social at most 1.1 {{SEXP_OK}}, and at least 10× faster at the largest size {{SPEED_OK}}. Building the largest world takes {{BUILD_B}} minutes. It holds {{BEL_B}} million beliefs in {{HEAP_B}} MB.

S1 · Design

Detail follows what the player could know

The player only ever sees the felt side of the world, so someone the player cannot reach needs to be true, not detailed. Everyone in the city is a full person, with traits, values, family and a seed. Only how often they are simulated differs.

in focus · monthly

Every system runs

The player's circle and two steps out, everyone who shares a household, workplace, class or group with them, and anyone a current story touches. Typically 1,000 to 3,000 people, a whole neighbourhood at a time.

in view · as counterparts

People in the room

A colleague at a big workplace or the bar staff at a regular venue. Full people form bonds with them and learn about them. They make no decisions of their own until promoted.

background · yearly

The coarse pass

They age, work, partner, have children, fall ill, leave, come home or die. The odds are fitted to the full engine and drawn on each person's own dice, and every big event goes into the record.

S3 · Background and promotion

A background learned from the engine

The background is not written by hand. Thirty full towns were logged year by year over twenty years. For each outcome, odds were fitted against what drives it in the engine: age, family, work, health, money, and the traits and values the engine reads. The fit was checked on held-out towns. One script refits them after any engine change, so the background follows the engine's own development.

Promotion: a neighbourhood comes into focus

When the player's story reaches someone, their whole neighbourhood comes into focus. Their record so far stands as it is. Routines are drawn from people their age in the monthly world. Then the years they actually lived there are replayed: home, family, work, school, circle, routines and the company they seek, by the engine's own contact rule. The clock is set back to when those months happened, so every rule that counts time sees months.

The cost

What the background does not keep

  • Small acts and their evidence. A promoted person arrives with friends and dislikes, but nobody has seen them do anything, so the player starts from nothing about them, as with any stranger.
  • Marks from small public moments. Being shown up, passed over or let down leaves sore spots in focus. In the background only big events leave marks: a death, a split, an illness, a homecoming.
  • Chains between background people. One person's grudge cannot make another move away. Only the fitted odds of big events act there.
  • Rivalries, collections and rows among background people, unless someone in focus is involved.
  • Friendships formed during catch-up enter the record dated to the promotion and marked as backstory, because the record must stay in time order.
For the engine

The scaling contract

Rules for new engine code, so it scales and a town stays exactly as it is. The full text is in the project, at claude/scale/contract.md.

  1. People meet through settings, never through the town. Household, workplace, school class, neighbourhood, venue, group, or the person's own ties.
  2. No loop over people inside a loop over people. A step for one person or one event takes others from that person's settings.
  3. Looking at a bond must not create one. Read with peekBond.
  4. News travels by setting and reach. For someone notable, reach will grow with fame, so fame becomes how far news about you travels.
  5. Read recent history from the end.
  6. Membership changes are tracked, so indexes stay right.
  7. Dice belong to people. A background life never depends on who else is simulated.
  8. Ids must fit a city. Bond keys break at 100,000 people and need a wider key first.
  9. The town stays the town. Every scaling change passes the identity test.
Method

How it was measured, and what was not

  • Two cores and 7 GB. Node 22 with tsx. Timed runs ran one at a time. Workplaces were copied in proportion to the town (one set of Skarvik's eight places per 46 founding adults).
  • A system's time is measured from the moment the monthly tick switches to its random stream to the next switch. The engine itself is not edited.
  • The branch is round 19c plus the changes listed in docs/branch-changes.md. Nothing in the lab's own files was touched. All work lives in its own folder and in claude/scale/.
  • Every expectation was written down before its runs (SCALE.md). The background was tuned on one 300-adult city (seed 1), which is excluded from the results above.

Not measured: speed in a browser, where the game may run; cities larger than about 3,000 people simulated monthly; memory over many decades; the in-view tier, which is designed but not built; a human cold read of promoted lives against always-full ones; and fame-driven reach.
