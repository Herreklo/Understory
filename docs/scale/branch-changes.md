# The scale branch: what it changes

The branch is round 19c with the changes below. Every change has one property: in a town of a single neighbourhood, it does exactly what the engine does, with the same arithmetic in the same order and the same dice. The identity test (bench/identity2.ts) checks this by hashing a whole run: every field of every person, every event, every bond and every belief, and the groups, workplaces and counters.

## New files

- **scale.ts — settings.**
  - Neighbourhoods: about 80 people each, fixed when the town is built. Households live together, and a household moves when people move in with each other.
  - A membership index: who is in town, staff by workplace, residents by neighbourhood, and who is jobless. A change of job updates it in place (`jobSet`). A change of presence rebuilds it (`moved()`).
  - Lookups: `staffOf` (a workplace's staff), `near` (the neighbourhoods of some people), `around` (someone's neighbourhood, workplace, circle, friends and family), `recent` (the last months of the record, read from the end).
  - Institutions: `makeInstances` gives a neighbourhood one workplace of each kind; `typeOf` is a workplace's kind; `placeOf` finds the instance of a kind in a neighbourhood; `hoodName` names a neighbourhood.
- **coarse.ts — the background of a city.**
  - The yearly pass. Person-local rules run by the engine's own formulas month by month: maturing (`mature`, shared with the engine's yearly step), health, illness, death, retirement, the person's own strain, and becoming a parent. Rules that depend on other people use odds from coarse-odds.ts: partnering, splitting, a child, leaving or coming home, drinking, losing a job, savings.
  - Background workplaces (`bgPlaces`): yearly hiring from the neighbourhood's job seekers, succession with the town's events (promotion, first staff meeting, passed over), and newcomers for slots nobody local can fill, by the town's newcomer rule.
  - Independence: ids from a range per neighbourhood (`bgId`), first names unique among the neighbourhood's residents, partners from the neighbourhood, every draw keyed to a person or a neighbourhood and year.
  - Background events carry the town's weight for their kind and how widely the town would have seen them (`seen`); a married couple that separates divorces.
  - The bonds a background person holds are put away with them (`stowBonds` in scale.ts) and come back on promotion; promotion changes nothing a person is, it only adds texture.
  - A city's background at its founding (`populateBackground`): households shaped like the town's founding households, with each neighbourhood's workplaces filled up to capacity.
  - Promotion of a neighbourhood (`promoteHood`), with its workplaces.
- **coarse-odds.ts** is generated by bench/fit.py from full-engine person-years. It is never edited by hand.

## Changes to engine files

- **Workplaces per neighbourhood (build.ts):** a city builds one set of workplaces per neighbourhood, and careers at year 0 are found in one's own neighbourhood. In a city split into focus and background (`SCALE_FOCUS`), background workplaces move to `bgPlaces` with their staff and leaders. The few engine references to a workplace by name now ask for a kind, in the person's neighbourhood (grammar.ts accidents, the harbour, the collection's bar; time.ts boat and bar; witness.ts; projects.ts threats; group.ts music teacher's school). In a town each kind has one instance with the kind's own id, so these find the same place.
- **Hiring in a city (life.ts `hire`):** a workplace takes on people from its own neighbourhood first, then from across the city. A venture opens in its founder's neighbourhood (projects.ts).
- **Maturing (life.ts):** the per-person body of the yearly step is the function `mature()`, so the background can share it. Same arithmetic, same order.
- **Generation (generate.ts):** `GenOpts` may give an id and the people a first name should differ from. Without them, generation is as before.
- **For tests (grammar.ts, build.ts):** `wiringC`, `identityC` and `pickExpression` are exported, so the twin test can pose the engine's own situations. `globalThis.SCALE_PAST_SALT` gives the monthly world a different past from the same founding, for the independence test. Neither changes a run.
- **Marks (persona.ts):** a mark reads an event's `seen` (how widely the town would have seen it) where it exists, else its `vis`. Only background events have `seen`.
- **Bond keys (world.ts):** `a × 2²⁶ + b` instead of `a × 100000 + b`, so a city of 100,000+ cannot collide. The map's order is unchanged, so a town's run is too.
- **`live()` (world.ts):** served from the membership index, the same people in the same order, so a city's background is never scanned.
- **Social tick (life.ts):**
  - Workplaces use staff lists. A vacancy draws from the jobless list. In a city it hears from at most 24 of the city's job seekers, drawn at random.
  - School, hobbies, routines and company sought happen within a person's neighbourhood.
  - Brothers and sisters are found through their parents, not by checking every pair.
  - Those who serve the public meet the adults of their place's neighbourhood.
  - Gossip reads only recent history.
- **News (world.ts):** witnesses at work come from staff lists. Public and local news reach the neighbourhoods of the people involved.
- **Situations, groups, projects, persona, drama, time:**
  - every scan of the town for a person's coworkers uses the staff list;
  - every scan for people who might react to someone uses the people around them;
  - the collection and the newcomer welcome read recent history only.
- **Bonds with strangers:** code that read a bond with `bond()` created an empty one for every person it looked at (project asking, cutting humour). It now looks only at the people around the person, so no bonds are created with strangers across a city.
- **Standing (regard.ts):** averaged over those around the person, not the whole town.
- **Attachments (time.ts):** run only for people in town or away, not for background people.
- **Status:** people can be `bg` (in the city, coarse pass) or `gone` (moved away from the city; the coarse pass may bring them home). The monthly systems act only on people `in` town, so they pass over both.
- **Build:**
  - neighbourhoods are set before circles, and circles are drawn from one's own neighbourhood;
  - a city can start with only some neighbourhoods in focus (`SCALE_FOCUS`), or as a town inside a background of a given size (`SCALE_CITY`);
  - the newcomer rule scales with the size of the monthly world (a town keeps its rule of 42 adults).
- **Romance (life.ts):** someone to fall for is someone in a person's settings, not anyone in the city.
- **Catch-up for promotion (life.ts, `catchUp`):**
  - The settings of the promoted are run on a clock set back to when those months happened, so time-counting rules see months. These cover home, family, work, school, circle, routines, hobbies, public service and company sought.
  - Who counts as close is refreshed each month, only for people whose bonds can have moved.
  - Anything written to the record in those months is dated to the promotion and marked as backstory.
